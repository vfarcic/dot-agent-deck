## Five more `src/spawn.rs` fixtures wait for the bytes they assert about, instead of guessing how long a PTY round trip takes

Each of the five asserted that something had physically **arrived** on a pane's PTY, and each was bounded by a fixed `tokio::time::sleep` rather than by the arrival itself — the defect [#850](https://github.com/vfarcic/dot-agent-deck/issues/850) and [#851](https://github.com/vfarcic/dot-agent-deck/issues/851) fixed, on paths neither reached. They are `#[cfg(test)]` fixtures, so no shipped behaviour changes here; what changes is that four blocking-check flakes and one silent false negative are gone from a tier with `retries = 0`, on the three required build jobs.

The conversions: `dispatch_018`'s attempt-2 precondition (800 ms) and the [#570](https://github.com/vfarcic/dot-agent-deck/issues/570) late-native-claim case (750 ms) now wait for their payload's own echo. `dispatch_020`'s delivery B and the plain-Enter positive control in `retry_after_user_frame` (75 ms each) need a new wait rather than the existing one: a **retry writes the same bytes its first attempt did**, by construction, so the existing content wait matches the earlier delivery's copies and returns having waited for nothing. `wait_for_further_payload_echo` counts copies from a floor the caller reads off its own baseline, which says *which* write it means; `wait_for_detached_payload_echo` is now that function with a floor of zero.

`retry_after_user_frame` was one sleep serving **two opposite contracts** — plain Enter must admit the replacement, Ctrl+J and Alt+Enter must not — so the polarity now travels with the call. The negative half keeps its sleep, because a contract that nothing arrives has no arrival to be keyed on, and is labelled as such.

`dispatch_016`'s case F is the one that failed **quietly**: a short read shrank the observed delta rather than panicking, so a delivery that wrote after being terminated would have read as the empty delta the case asserts — green, and wrong. Its delivery task is joined before the window opens, so nothing but the fixture can still write; the window is now closed by a **fence write** whose echo the fixture waits for. The PTY preserves order and the buffer is append-only, so anything the delivery wrote is already ahead of the fence, and the observation stops depending on a threshold at all.

Measured the way #850 and #851 were, on 16 cores with `SUBMIT_DELAY` set to 0 to remove the incidental head start those two 75 ms sites were living on. Shrunk, the assertions fail: at 4x oversubscription plus a fork storm, `dispatch_020` failed **2 runs in 12** — once on delivery B, once on the plain-Enter control; at 20x, with the two long sleeps cut to 505 ms, the [#570](https://github.com/vfarcic/dot-agent-deck/issues/570) case failed **11 of 12** and `dispatch_018`'s precondition **9 of 12**. With the waits in place all three tests passed **12 of 12** at 20x. Case F is the honest exception: an injected stand-in for a late write was caught 12 of 12 even with its window cut tenfold to 25 ms, so its failure mode is argued from the code rather than reproduced by load — the fence is a class fix, not a measured flake.

Out of scope and deliberately untouched: the negative-observation windows where the sleep **is** the measurement, the `#[cfg(windows)]` frame sleep [#854](https://github.com/vfarcic/dot-agent-deck/issues/854) left unverified, the 100 ms that must land inside production's 500 ms retry window (that one needs `tokio::time::pause` and its own issue), and `xtask/linkage-check`'s sleep-rule exemption for `src/` ([#850](https://github.com/vfarcic/dot-agent-deck/issues/850) step 3).
