## Voice control in the desktop app

The desktop app can now be driven by voice. Press **Voice**, say *"show me the overview"* or *"open the tester"* or *"go back"*, and the app does it — and says what it did. The button stays on and keeps listening: say the next command when you are ready, and press it again when you are done. The button itself says which it is, **Voice on** or **Voice off**, so there is never a question about whether the microphone is open.

**You do not press anything between commands.** The app works out where one utterance ends by listening for you to stop speaking — about eight tenths of a second of quiet — and then transcribes that much and nothing else. Speak again and the next one starts.

**Speaking needs a key today, and pressing Voice tells you so.** Transcription starts **Off**, and pressing the button with nothing configured shows you where to go — Settings → Voice — rather than doing nothing or greying itself out. Pick a speech backend, add a key, press Voice again, and it works; you do not need to restart the app.

**It works with nothing configured for the second half, on a machine that already has an agent CLI.** Turning a sentence into an action needs a model, and the default uses the `claude` CLI you are already signed in to rather than asking you for an API key or downloading anything. That costs latency — three to five seconds — so the app shows you the number rather than leaving you looking at a spinner wondering whether it hung. If you would rather have the speed, Settings → Voice switches to a keyed remote service that answers in about a second; you supply the key, and it goes to your operating system's keychain rather than into any settings file.

**What it can do today is navigation, and only navigation.** Open one agent's pane, open the agent overview, go back to the deck, close an agent view, open settings. Nothing that starts an agent, stops one, or answers a permission prompt is reachable by voice, and that is deliberate rather than unfinished — those are the commands where a rare misfire is expensive, and they are waiting on a confirmation step of their own. "Close the agent view" closes the *view*; it does not stop the agent and does not close its terminal pane.

**Asking for something it cannot do gets you a useful answer rather than a shrug.** Ask to close an agent view when none is open and it tells you what it needs first, naming the prerequisite. Ask for something it has no command for and it says so and **shows you exactly what it heard**, so a mis-transcription is something you can correct instead of a mystery. Every sentence you see comes from the app, not from the model — the model picks an action from a fixed list and never writes prose you read. After a command that moved you somewhere, an **Undo** sits in the report for ten seconds.

**Talk for thirty seconds without pausing and it throws that away rather than charging you for it.** A command here is one to four words, so half a minute of unbroken speech is a conversation in the room or a microphone pointed at a call — never a command. The app says so briefly and keeps listening, and no transcription or model call is spent on it.

**Nothing you say is written down by this app.** No transcript, utterance or audio buffer is logged or saved anywhere, and the recording is turned into text before it ever leaves the Rust side of the app. Two limits on that, stated because the unqualified version would be false: when the default backend hands your sentence to the `claude` CLI, it passes the flag that tells that program not to keep a session — so the guarantee there is one this app *asks for* from another program. And if you choose a keyed remote backend, your words go to that service, and what it keeps is its policy and not something this app can promise anything about.

**Known limits worth reading before you rely on it.** There is one activation mode — press on, press off, with the app finding the end of each utterance in between — and hold-to-talk is still to come. The microphone is closed while a command is being transcribed and worked out, so speech during those few seconds is not captured; the report says which of the two it is doing. Speech-to-text is a hosted service until local transcription lands, which is what would remove the key requirement entirely. And the **microphone permission prompt has not been exercised on a packaged build on any platform**: the app carries the macOS usage description it needs, but no automated test in this project can open a real microphone or drive the real app window, so the first time you press that button is genuinely the first time.

The desktop app remains an unsigned alpha outside the CLI's support expectations. Maintainer notes — the pipeline, the voice-activity detection that segments it, the command table and its three consumers, what adding a command actually costs, what the build-time guard does and does not see, and the manual checks that cover what no test does — are in `docs/develop/desktop-gui.md`, which is maintainer documentation and is not published to the docs site.
