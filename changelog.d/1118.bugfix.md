## A daemon shutdown can no longer wait forever on a child that will not finish exiting

`AgentPtyRegistry::force_kill_and_reap_all` — the last phase of every daemon teardown, reached from the idle shutdown, from a SIGTERM, from `daemon stop`'s `KIND_SHUTDOWN` frame and from the registry's own `Drop` — SIGKILLed every managed agent and then polled `try_wait` until each one reported an exit status. There was no deadline on that poll and no log line anywhere in it, so a child that never reported an exit parked the shutdown indefinitely and presented as a hang with nothing in `deck.log` to say what it was waiting for. The reap now has a deadline, and when it expires the daemon logs which children it could not reap — by pid and pane — and finishes shutting down instead of waiting.

**This is defensive hardening of an unbounded wait, not a fix for a demonstrated production hang, and the distinction is worth keeping.** The condition was measured in a *test*: [#959](https://github.com/vfarcic/dot-agent-deck/issues/959) found 18 of 4500 executions on `macos-latest` stalled in exactly this loop with the child in `ps` state `?Es` ("trying to exit"), and [#1117](https://github.com/vfarcic/dot-agent-deck/pull/1117) fixed the test's own construction — a PTY with both ends held and neither read, which the production spawn path never has. No production path has been shown to reach it, and none is claimed here. What is true either way is that "how long may this block?" had no answer, and now it does.

**Giving up is a choice with a cost, so here is the cost.** The children have already been SIGKILLed by the preceding pass, so nothing the deck can still do makes one exit sooner — waiting only moves who notices. Nothing downstream reads the collected statuses. And every production caller is on a teardown path, so a child abandoned on Unix is re-parented to init (or the nearest subreaper) as soon as the process exits and reaped there, while on Windows the handle closes with the process. The give-up therefore costs one process-table entry for the remainder of the daemon's life, and it never abandons the *kill* — only the status collection.

**The deadline is derived, not picked round.** `daemon stop` and the build-mismatch prompt each ask the daemon to stop and then poll five seconds for its listener to go away before escalating (`daemon stop --force` SIGKILLs the daemon) or reporting a timeout. Of that window the teardown can already spend the three-second SIGTERM grace, and those two costs co-occur in precisely the interesting case — a child that ignored SIGTERM is the child the next phase has to SIGKILL and reap. The reap gets half of what is left, with the other half as headroom for the daemon to actually exit, and compile-time assertions pin the relationship rather than leaving it to a comment. So bounding the wait gives up no reap that waiting would have won: past that outer window the reap was abandoned anyway, by a layer that does not know which children it abandoned and cannot say so.

Nothing crosses the TUI↔daemon protocol — the change is confined to one in-process teardown function that touches no frame, field or handler — so `PROTOCOL_VERSION` is untouched.
