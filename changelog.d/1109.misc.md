## An unguarded daemon shutdown now records what it destroyed

There are four ways to ask a `dot-agent-deck` daemon to stop, and only two of them can be refused. `dot-agent-deck daemon stop` and the `StopDaemon` wire verb refuse while the daemon holds orchestration roles whose panes still have a live agent, because those role registrations live in the daemon's memory and nowhere else — an agent that survives the stop keeps running and keeps posting hook events, but can never delegate again. A plain `SIGTERM` and the TUI's `Stop` button got none of that: both produced a compliant graceful shutdown that stopped every managed agent while naming none of them, so establishing what a stray `pkill -f "daemon serve"` had just taken down meant reading back through the log by hand.

Those two paths still do not refuse, deliberately — a `SIGTERM` is how a service manager, a container runtime or a session logout asks a daemon to stop, and a daemon that argues back is escalated to `SIGKILL` on the sender's clock, which loses the graceful drain *and* any chance to leave a record. What they do now is say what they are taking down: one line in `deck.log`, before the shutdown drains anything, naming which path fired, how many agents are being terminated and how many role registrations destroyed, every live pane with its label and working directory, every orchestration role with its orchestration and which pane is the orchestrator, and that those registrations are gone for good.

The reasoning, including the two alternatives that were rejected — discriminating on the signal's sender, and hiding the daemon's command line from `pkill -f` — is recorded in `docs/develop/daemon-teardown-paths.md`. No protocol change: the wire shape, the drain, its grace windows and the exit statuses are unchanged — what is new is a log line in front of them, bounded so it cannot push a shutdown past what `daemon stop` waits for.
