## Answer an Agent's Questions by Voice

When an agent stops to ask you something — a permission prompt, a menu of options, or a form of several questions — you can now answer it by voice in the desktop app. Open the agent's pane and say it the way you would pick it on the keyboard: the number beside the option ("2", "two", "the second one"), its words or a part only it has ("No", "auto mode"), or what you mean ("go ahead"), with "um" or "please" around it if you like. A whole label wins over a longer one that starts with it, so "yes" is "Yes" and not "Yes, and switch to auto mode"; words that start several options choose the one that allows once when just one does, so "yes" to Codex is "Yes, proceed"; other words that fit several options choose nothing, and the row names the options they could be, with their numbers. Where Claude Code may or may not show an option, the app doesn't guess the numbers of the options after it: say those by name, such as "no". A form is answered question by question, by number or by words, and the row names the next question's options with their numbers; "red for colour and large for size" in one sentence works too. On a question that takes several answers, name them together ("one and three", "small and large"), and for an option that asks for your own words, choose it and then say them. Typing mode does not get in the way: with it on, saying an option's number or words answers the question, nothing else is typed while the question waits, and typing goes on once it has been answered. The answer is shown with a five-second countdown that names the option it will send, which stops as soon as the app hears you speak, and nothing is sent if the question was answered or changed in the meantime, or if the agent's terminal is not on screen. Choosing an "always allow" option first opens a confirmation that says exactly what it will always allow; press Confirm or say "confirm" — a plain "yes" does not confirm it. Read the countdown before it ends: it shows exactly what will be sent, and the app can pick the wrong option for words that were about something else. While a question is waiting, voice neither types into the agent's pane nor presses `Enter` there, since a key in the question can choose an option: "type …", "send it", "enter" or "go ahead" said without answering it does nothing, and the row lists the options you can say, with their numbers, or says to use the keyboard.

With reading on, you hear the question first, with the numbers you can answer with — "The coder is asking for permission: Bash: cargo publish. Say 1 for Yes, or say No." — and then answer it by voice from that agent's pane. Answer once the app has finished speaking: what is said while the app speaks is not taken as an answer.

This works for Claude Code 2.1.136 or newer, Codex and OpenCode, including their multiple-choice questions and forms, and for Pi, for the dialogs other Pi extensions show. Devin's permission prompts are supported from its documentation but have not been tried with a running Devin. A few options still have to be answered with the keyboard, such as Claude Code's "Chat about this" and Codex's "None of the above"; the docs list them per agent. After upgrading, run `dot-agent-deck hooks install` (and `dot-agent-deck hooks install --agent codex` for Codex), or restart the daemon, and restart agents that were already running.

While an agent's question is waiting, what you say that the app does not match to an option itself is sent to the Commands service together with that question — its text, its options and what it is about, which for a permission prompt is usually the command the agent wants to run — whether **Send on-screen names** is on or off.

An OpenCode subagent's question is shown as the subagent's and answered the same way. Codex's multiple-choice questions and OpenCode's question menus now show **Needs Input** on a TUI card while they wait for you, instead of **Working**, and a Pi card shows **Needs Input** while a dialog from another Pi extension waits. In the desktop app those agents show **needs input** instead of **running**.

See [Voice Control](https://agent-deck.devopstoolkit.ai/docs/desktop/voice.md) for details.
