A daemon left with nothing to do now reliably shuts itself down. Previously it could get stuck running indefinitely: the idle window is armed as a one-shot timer, and if that timer happened to reach its deadline during a moment when the daemon was busy — a TUI connecting, an agent starting or finishing, a schedule being reloaded — it correctly declined to shut down, but nothing ever armed another one. From then on the daemon was immortal. It did not matter how long it sat idle afterwards, or that nothing could reach it any more: an orphan whose sockets had been unlinked and whose working tree had been deleted was observed still running 21 hours later, with no clients, no agents and no pending schedules, holding a pid and its state directory. Each such survivor costs little on its own, but nothing bounded how many could accumulate — most likely to bite where daemons are started and discarded in quick succession, such as cleaning up a worktree while its daemon is still running. The idle timer now hands arming back when it declines, so the daemon re-checks and exits once it is genuinely idle again.

Only the retry is new: what counts as idle is unchanged, and a shutdown still requires no clients, no live agents and no registered enabled schedule at the moment the window elapses. Setting `DOT_AGENT_DECK_IDLE_SHUTDOWN_SECS=0` still disables idle shutdown entirely for an always-on daemon, and a registered enabled schedule still keeps a daemon alive between fires.
