## Delegating to a `clear = true` role while its pane is closing no longer kills that role for the session

An orchestration role marked `clear = true` gets a fresh worker on every delegate: the daemon disposes of the current one and starts a replacement. If that delegate landed while the same pane was being closed — you pressed `Ctrl+w` on the worker, or a dispatch tore it down, and the orchestrator delegated a moment later — the replacement was never made. The role ended up with no agent at all, its pane vanished from `dot-agent-deck daemon status`, and every later delegate to it was rejected with `reached no worker for role(s)`. Nothing brought it back short of recreating the orchestration. That is the failure [#606](https://github.com/vfarcic/dot-agent-deck/issues/606) was filed about, and the recovery added for it turned out to have two ways past it.

**The delegate could destroy the pane's record on its way to failing.** A close marks the pane and reserves it *before* the step that removes its registry entry, and those two are separated by a thread hop. A delegate landing in between found an entry, took the ordinary replace-this-worker path — which lifts the entry out and terminates its child before the daemon refuses to start anything on a pane that is being cleaned up — and that refusal was not the one that routes to #606's recovery. So the role was left empty *and* its entry was gone, which also left the close itself failing against an agent that was no longer there. The replace path now declines a reserved pane before it touches anything, under the same lock it would have removed the entry under, and reports it in the form that reaches the recovery.

There is a mirror image of that, and it needed the same treatment from the other side: a close that read the worker's record a moment *before* the delegate removed it takes its reservation a moment *after*, so the ordinary replace path — already committed, worker already terminated — met the refusal on its way out rather than on its way in. That now enters the same recovery instead of being reported as a failure, and the role comes back once the close lets go.

**And a slow close could outlast the recovery's patience.** The recovery waited six seconds for the close to release the pane and then, in its own words, attempted the spawn anyway — which cannot work while the pane is still reserved, so crossing that line was not a degraded outcome but the whole failure. It was reachable: measured at one run in 120 with 80 copies of the same scenario on a 16-core box, and once inside an ordinary test run at default parallelism. Six seconds now only marks the point at which the daemon log says the close is slow; the recovery keeps waiting out to thirty, which is a wedged-daemon backstop rather than an estimate of a loaded close, and is the same order the delegate path already spends waiting for a *successful* replacement to announce itself. The orchestrator's `delegate` has already returned by then, so nobody is sitting at a prompt waiting; what the longer wait does hold back is another delegate to that same pane, or a `pane restart` of it, both of which are operations on a pane whose close is still in flight. So the trade is a delayed delegate on a closing pane, against a role lost for the rest of the session.

`pane restart <role>` runs through the same recovery and gets the same repair. Its CLI reply budget deliberately still covers only the healthy-daemon case, so a restart that meets a starved close can still report "still in flight and may have already succeeded" — the pre-existing advice for exactly that residue — rather than making every restart wait out a ceiling for a case that is nearly never hit.

Nothing about the TUI↔daemon protocol moved: no request or response shape changed, no field changed meaning, and `PROTOCOL_VERSION` is untouched. What changed is how long one daemon-internal operation waits before giving up, and what it does with a pane it may not touch yet — neither of which is observable across a version boundary.
