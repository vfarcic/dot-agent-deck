## `delegate/034` waits for a gap that is really there, instead of one that closed 2.57 ms after it opened

`orchestration/delegate/034` failed intermittently on its **precondition** — `the respawn never lifted the pane's record out` — at roughly 1 run in 12 on an idle 16-core box, and twice in a row on CI runners, which is what left `main` red. The message was false in every failure measured. The respawn had lifted the record; it had also published a replacement **2.57 ms** later, and the 5 ms poll looking for the gap stepped over it, spent the rest of its budget looking at the *successor's* record, and reported it as the original's. Issue #1148 records a bisect built on that sentence, which produced a nonsense culprit.

**The product was never at fault.** What was wrong is the test's premise that a `trap '' TERM` stand-in makes the respawn spend its full three-second `AGENT_TERMINATE_GRACE` between lifting the pane's record out and spawning the replacement. `respawn_agent_for_pane_declared` deliberately drops the PTY writer and master **before** it terminates, so that I/O blocked on the PTY unblocks first — and closing the master hands the old child EOF on its stdin. A `cat` then exits **0** of its own accord, with the SIGTERM it traps never mattering at all, and `terminate_child_with_grace_and_wait` returns after one 50 ms `try_wait` tick. Measured on this path, the old child's exit status is `(success, 0)` and never a signal. So the gap was ~50 ms rather than ~3 s — and collapsed to ~2.5 ms whenever the child's exit landed before the terminate helper's *immediate first* `try_wait`, a sub-millisecond scheduling race, which is why the failure was load-sensitive in the IO direction rather than despite it.

`close_agent` is the mirror image: it terminates while still **holding** the whole `RunningAgent`, master included, so a SIGTERM-ignoring `cat` really does survive to the SIGKILL backstop there. `/022` and `close_pane_into_its_grace_window` depend on that and are unaffected; only the respawn leg has the asymmetry, and only `/034` waits on it.

**The fix makes the gap real rather than making the wait longer.** `/034`'s worker now ignores TERM *and* HUP and reads nothing at all (`trap '' TERM HUP; exec sleep 300`), so losing the PTY is not an event for it and no signal the grace phase sends can end it: it survives to the SIGKILL backstop and the gap is `AGENT_TERMINATE_GRACE` wide **by construction**. Measured at **3.02 s** against the 5 ms poll — 600x the cadence, up from 50 ms — and the poll is sound for the reason its sibling documents: while the respawn is parked in its terminate it is the only thing that can publish onto the pane, so "no record for this pane" cannot be left until the respawn itself leaves it.

**No registry signal for the lift edge was added, and that was a choice rather than an omission.** A signal would make the edge unmissable, and unmissable is not what this test needs — it needs to *be inside* the gap when it places the hold, not to learn afterwards that the gap happened. Against the 2.57 ms gap that failed, a signal-woken test would have lost the same race and simply failed elsewhere. The only signal that fixes it is one that HOLDS the respawn between the lift and the spawn, i.e. a test-only barrier in a hot product path. The width of the gap was the defect; the sampling was only how it surfaced.

One incidental improvement, measured rather than assumed: `sleep 300` bounds the leak if a replacement worker ever outlives its harness, where `cat` blocked on a PTY would not. An orphaned replacement was observed once across ~130 runs, with its parent already gone, and it exited on its own inside the 300 s.

**The precondition keeps its teeth, and gained the ones it was missing.** It still refuses to proceed unless the gap is genuinely open — the assertion underneath would pass for the wrong reason otherwise, which is why `/032` deliberately has no early exit on "the record has gone" either. What is new is that the two failures it used to merge are now told apart: a record under an agent id that is *not* the one the delegate was aimed at is proof the respawn ran the whole gap unobserved, reported as such and named with the successor's id, while the pane still sitting on the delegated id after the budget means the dispatch task never reached the respawn. Forced deterministically, the old wait reports the false sentence after 24.2 s; the new one reports the real cause in **0.09 s**.

The gap wait is also bounded by a new `common::daemon_task_start_budget` rather than `child_boot_budget`, because what it waits on is a freshly `tokio::spawn`ed dispatch task getting its turn, not a child producing its first byte. Same 8 s base and the same load scaling, so the rename changes no behaviour — but the old name is how a wait sized for the wrong event went unquestioned. `close_pane_into_its_grace_window`, which named the right quantity in prose while borrowing the same wrong constant, moves with it.

Verified in both directions. Post-fix: **87 consecutive passes of `delegate_034`, zero failures** — two deliberately loaded 40-run batches on 16 cores (the first at load average 23–35 with `/proc/pressure/io` `full avg60` reaching **62.6**, the second at 15.8–22.9), plus `cargo test-fast`, `cargo test-e2e`, three whole-file runs and CI's own `e2e-deterministic`. Load matters here because this class fails **under** IO pressure rather than despite it, so an idle-box green is the weakest evidence available — which is also why the deterministic measurement carries more weight than the count: the gap is 3.02 s where it was 50 ms with a sub-5 ms tail.

The natural failure was reproduced once, on the first instrumented run, and could not be re-hit on demand afterwards (0 in ~110 further runs across several load conditions) — consistent with the issue's 1-in-12 being a tail event. So the "before" side is demonstrated deterministically instead, by forcing the collapsed gap with `grace = Duration::ZERO`, which is precisely what "the terminate helper's first `try_wait` already saw the exit" achieves: the old wait reports its false sentence after **24.2 s** at a 4.22 ms gap, and the new one names the real cause and the successor id in **0.09 s** at a 3.04 ms gap.

And with `respawn_or_recreate_agent_for_pane`'s `DuplicatePaneId if pane_held_for_cleanup` arm removed — issue #1114's own defect restored — the test still goes red on its **assertion** rather than its precondition, at 25.14 s, matching the figure that entry's own "Verified load-bearing" line already recorded. So the precondition passes and the property it guards is still guarded.

No product behaviour changed: the only non-test edit is a comment at the respawn's `drop(master)` recording that the grace it spends is bounded by how the child reacts to losing its PTY, not by what signals it traps.
