## Fast-Tier Tests Survive a Busy Machine

Three fast-tier tests waited a fixed wall-clock deadline — 2 s, 5 s, or a pair of flat sleeps — for a freshly spawned stand-in process to reach a state the test depends on. On a contended box the child has simply not been scheduled inside that window, so `orchestration/delegate/012`, `orchestration/delegate/022` and `scheduler/idle-worker/008` failed with a starvation signature that is indistinguishable from a real delivery defect. Measured in the report: 3, 2 and 1 failures respectively across a 12-run streak of the full fast tier on a 16-core box at load average 44, against 12/12 clean on the same commit at load 4–9.

Every one of those waits is now driven by the state it is actually waiting for. `orchestration/delegate/022`, which had two of them, waits for its SIGTERM-ignoring stand-in to print a marker proving its `trap '' TERM` is installed, and then for the pane's close to observably enter its termination grace — rather than sleeping 400 ms and 200 ms and hoping both have happened. The remaining boot waits keep polling for the child's own output but take their ceiling from how contended the machine is, and end early if the child dies rather than prints — so an idle box still fails as promptly as it did, and a loaded one stops reporting "not scheduled yet" as "produced the wrong thing".

No production code changed, and no assertion was widened, relaxed, or dropped: the negative windows in which something must *not* happen keep their original fixed lengths, because their length is part of what they assert.
