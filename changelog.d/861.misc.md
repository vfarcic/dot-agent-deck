## The e2e lifetime cap now survives the owning process's death

`DOT_AGENT_DECK_TEST_MAX_LIFETIME_SECS` bounds how long anything a test spawns may live. It is read in exactly three places — the daemon's backstop task, `wrap`'s self-defence, and the forked reaper `wrap` arms for its child — and each of them bounds the process it runs in, then reaches everything *else* by signalling a **process group**. A `killpg` reaches nothing that has left the group, and agents put processes outside it as a matter of routine: measured live, Claude Code's Bash tool runs every shell it executes in a session of its own. Such a process inherited the cap and was bounded by nothing at all. Measured on the shared dev box: one carrying `DOT_AGENT_DECK_TEST_MAX_LIFETIME_SECS=300` in its own environment, reparented to `init`, alive **4 days 01:23** — about 1170x its cap — with its owning test process dead and its temp root already deleted from under it.

The deadline is now enforced by something a process group cannot hide from. Both spawn seams export a per-spawn `DOT_AGENT_DECK_TEST_LIFETIME_TAG` beside the cap, and the reaper sweeps `/proc/<pid>/environ` for it at the deadline in addition to `killpg`ing the group. A tag survives what a group cannot: `execve` passes the caller's environment on by default, so it reaches descendants of every shape unless one deliberately rebuilds its own, and it is still readable after the whole ancestry is gone — which is the state the reported orphan was found in. Group death therefore no longer ends the reaper; it triggers one sweep, after which the reaper exits immediately if nothing carries the tag or waits out its deadline and ends what does. Three things keep a sweep from reaching further than it should: inheritance is the only route a tag travels, the needle is matched as an exact NUL-delimited environment *entry* rather than a substring (so a tag that is a prefix of another does not match it), and no two tags held by *live* processes collide — pid separates concurrent spawners, a per-process counter separates their panes, and `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` separates a reused pid from its predecessor. That last guarantee is deliberately scoped to live processes: the clock restarts at boot, so cross-boot collisions are possible and inert, since no process outlives the boot it was tagged on.

It is armed at `agent_pty::spawn` rather than only in `wrap`, and that is the half that matters for the orphan actually measured: only `Wrapper`-strategy agents are wrapped, so Codex has a reaper and Claude Code — which is `NativeHooks`, and whose Bash-tool shell this was — is spawned bare. One call at the common spawn boundary covers wrapped and unwrapped agents, the daemon's panes, the TUI's, and a test's in-process registry alike.

Two things it does not do, both deliberate. The sweep needs `/proc/<pid>/environ`, so it is Linux-only and a no-op elsewhere, leaving the pre-existing group half unchanged — the same platform split `wrap`'s reaper already has between `close_range(2)` and a portable `close` loop. And nothing here survives a `SIGKILL` aimed at the reaper itself, which is the residual that reaper has always carried: it leaves its parent's process group so a group kill cannot take it, but a direct signal still can, and then the deadline has no holder.

Gated exactly as the rest of this machinery is: no tag is minted, exported, or swept unless `DOT_AGENT_DECK_TEST_MAX_LIFETIME_SECS` names a cap, which the test harness sets and production does not.

`clean-e2e-tmp`'s dead-owner floor was re-checked against this and is **unchanged** — the reasoning is in [`docs/develop/e2e-temp-dirs.md`](https://github.com/vfarcic/dot-agent-deck/blob/main/docs/develop/e2e-temp-dirs.md). Its derivation used to read "an orphan can keep writing under the dead test's root for up to that cap", which held for the class it named and not for an escapee. The number was still right for two compounding reasons: no finite floor bounds an unbounded writer, and the floor never protected that class in the first place — the reaper reads liveness off the *test* process's PID in the root's name and never off an orphan, so an escapee has never held its own root against `--apply` at any age. So `MAX_PINNED_ORPHAN_CAP_SECS` and `DEAD_PID_MIN_AGE` are untouched, linkage-check rule 11 is untouched, and what changed is the claim.
