## One agent's terminal, at window size, in the desktop app

The desktop app could show you nine agents at tile size and could not show you one agent's terminal large. Reading the one agent that mattered right now meant reading a ninth of the window, and the two partial escapes did not close the gap: the Reader overlay is reading-width but is a *text* projection with no cursor, no colour and no keyboard into the agent, and the fleet overview told you which agent you wanted and then had nowhere to send you. Clicking the maximise control on a deck tile — or on an agent's row in the overview — now opens **that agent's pane over the screen you were on**, at the full size of the window, with its terminal live and typeable. `Esc` or the close control puts you back exactly where you came from, on the same panel tab, with the deck's scroll position and selection intact.

**It is the same pane you were already looking at, not a second one.** The grid tile and the full-window pane are one component rendered at two presentations, so all five panel tabs (terminal, diff, checks, handoffs, artifacts) are there at both sizes and the tab you were on travels with you in both directions. The terminal keeps its scrollback, its selection and its cursor across opening and closing, because the tile's own terminal is promoted in place rather than rebuilt in a new container.

**Enlarging really enlarges.** The pane asks the daemon for a bigger PTY rather than scaling a small one up, so the agent's own output re-lays-out to the wider grid instead of sitting in a corner at its old column count. That has a visible consequence for anyone else attached to the same agent: a PTY has one window size, negotiated as the smallest reported by any attached viewer, so a TUI user watching the same agent will see their pane reflow when a desktop user opens the pane and reflow back when it closes. Nothing is truncated for them — the smallest-wins rule puts every viewer that has reported its geometry on the padding side of a mismatch — but the reflow is real and it arrives with nothing on their screen explaining why.

**Opening over the deck costs nothing.** The grid stays mounted and attached underneath, so no terminal is detached, nothing is re-attached, and no agent replays its scrollback when you close the pane. Opening from the overview costs exactly one attach, which is the minimum possible there: the overview deliberately holds no terminals at all, so it goes from zero attached to one.

**Opening an agent that belongs to a deck you are not currently on works, and it is the expensive path.** The app switches the selected deck to that agent's deck first and switches back when you close — which writes your deck selection to `desktop.toml` and tears down every terminal session in each direction, so the first cross-deck open reads as slow where a same-deck open is instant. A clean open-and-close puts your deck selection back exactly as it was, and every configured deck with it — but the file itself is rewritten rather than restored, so comments and hand formatting in `desktop.toml` do not survive your first cross-deck open. If the app is killed while a cross-deck pane is open, the saved selection stays moved and the app comes back on the other deck. Where two configured decks are described identically — differing only in socket path, identity file or jump host — nothing is switched and the pane's terminal does not attach, deliberately, because selecting the wrong row would attach a different machine's agent under the right name.

**Two controls are absent from the overview-origin pane, and that is a known asymmetry rather than an oversight.** A pane opened from the overview offers no rename and no evidence-drawer selection, while the same pane opened from the deck offers both. The evidence drawer belongs to the deck screen, and no deck is mounted underneath an overview-origin pane that could open it; a rename reports its outcome through the deck's toast, so offering it there would let a failed rename pass silently. Both would need the overview to grow a notice surface of its own.

The Reader overlay is still reachable from a tile, and it is not offered inside the full-window pane, where it would compete for the same `Esc` key. One change to it: a Reader you left open on a tile is **dismissed** when you open a pane over that screen — including a pane on a different agent. It would otherwise sit invisible behind the pane and answer your `Esc` along with it, closing both.

Developer notes — the one-component rule, the attach arithmetic, the cross-deck cost and the extended manual smoke check — are in `docs/develop/desktop-gui.md`, which is maintainer documentation and is not published to the docs site.
