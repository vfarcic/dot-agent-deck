## One agent's terminal, at window size, in the desktop app

The desktop app could show you nine agents at tile size and could not show you one agent's terminal large. Reading the one agent that mattered right now meant reading a ninth of the window, and the two partial escapes did not close the gap: the Reader overlay is reading-width but is a *text* projection with no cursor, no colour and no keyboard into the agent, and the fleet overview told you which agent you wanted and then had nowhere to send you. Clicking the maximise control on a deck tile — or anywhere on an agent's row in the overview — now opens **that agent's pane over the screen you were on**, at the full size of the window, with its terminal live and typeable. `Esc` or the close control puts you back exactly where you came from, on the same panel tab, with the deck's scroll position and selection intact.

**It is the same pane you were already looking at, not a second one.** The grid tile and the full-window pane are one component rendered at two presentations, so all five panel tabs (terminal, diff, checks, handoffs, artifacts) are there at both sizes and the tab you were on travels with you in both directions. The terminal keeps its scrollback, its selection and its cursor across opening and closing, because the tile's own terminal is promoted in place rather than rebuilt in a new container.

**Enlarging really enlarges.** The pane asks the daemon for a bigger PTY rather than scaling a small one up, so the agent's own output re-lays-out to the wider grid instead of sitting in a corner at its old column count. That has a visible consequence for anyone else attached to the same agent: a PTY has one window size, negotiated as the smallest reported by any attached viewer, so a TUI user watching the same agent will see their pane reflow when a desktop user opens the pane and reflow back when it closes. Nothing is truncated for them — the smallest-wins rule puts every viewer that has reported its geometry on the padding side of a mismatch — but the reflow is real and it arrives with nothing on their screen explaining why.

**Opening over the deck costs nothing.** The grid stays mounted and attached underneath, so no terminal is detached, nothing is re-attached, and no agent replays its scrollback when you close the pane. Opening an agent from the overview costs exactly one attach, which is the minimum possible there: the overview deliberately holds no terminals at all, so it goes from zero attached to one.

**Every agent the overview lists opens with a live terminal, whichever deck it is on.** The overview shows every deck it can see at once, and the maximise control is on every row — including the rows belonging to a deck you are not currently looking at. Opening one of those gives you the same working pane as any other: output arriving from that machine, a keyboard into that agent, and its own resize. **Your deck selection does not move**, and nothing writes `desktop.toml`. That is the point of the screen — one place from which any agent on any deck can be worked with, which is what the desktop app offers over a TUI bound to a single deck.

Under it, the pane carries the deck and the agent together everywhere the app used to carry the agent alone. Agent ids are only unique within one daemon, so two decks routinely run a `planner`; the pane's terminal, its keystrokes, its resize and its recorded state are all addressed by the pair, and the app opens the stream against that agent's own deck rather than against whichever deck happens to be selected when the request lands.

**A deck the app cannot reach is the one case that still has no terminal, and the pane says which deck and why.** If the deck an open pane belongs to stops answering, the terminal is replaced by a plain statement naming that deck and repeating its own report of what went wrong — a sentence rather than a black rectangle, because a terminal that will never receive anything looks exactly like an agent that has gone quiet. When the deck answers again the terminal comes back in the pane you already have, with no reopening.

An earlier build of this feature switched your selected deck on your behalf and switched back on close. That was withdrawn before release: moving the selection under an open pane let state created on one deck be attributed to another, and the pane reaching its own deck directly is what made the switch unnecessary rather than merely unwise.

**Two controls are absent from the overview-origin pane, and that is a known asymmetry rather than an oversight.** A pane opened from the overview offers no rename and no evidence-drawer selection, while the same pane opened from the deck offers both. The evidence drawer belongs to the deck screen, and no deck is mounted underneath an overview-origin pane that could open it; a rename reports its outcome through the deck's toast, so offering it there would let a failed rename pass silently. Both would need the overview to grow a notice surface of its own.

The Reader overlay is still reachable from a tile, and it is not offered inside the full-window pane, where it would compete for the same `Esc` key. One change to it: a Reader you left open on a tile is **dismissed** when you open a pane over that screen — including a pane on a different agent. It would otherwise sit invisible behind the pane and answer your `Esc` along with it, closing both.

One related change you may notice if you run several decks: **switching decks no longer detaches every terminal.** It used to, because every attach went to whichever deck was selected. A terminal now ends when its own deck leaves your configured fleet, when that deck's daemon is stopped or replaced, or when the app exits — so a pane you deliberately opened on another machine survives you looking at something else.

Developer notes — the one-component rule, the attach arithmetic, the composite deck identity and the extended manual smoke check — are in `docs/develop/desktop-gui.md`, which is maintainer documentation and is not published to the docs site.
