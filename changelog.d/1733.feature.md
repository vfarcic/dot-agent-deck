## Voice commands understood by meaning

In the desktop app, voice now goes by what you mean rather than by particular words. Previously a command ran only when your sentence contained one of the words the app expected, so "destroy the tester" was refused with "nothing in that asks to stop an agent" even though it plainly asks for that. Now "destroy the tester" and "get rid of the tester agent" open the same Stop confirmation as "stop the tester", "throw away this draft" discards the New agent form, and "narrate future agent updates aloud" turns reading on. The phrases in the docs are examples, not a list to learn; typing mode still acts only on the phrases it lists, because everything else is typed.

Before a command runs, the app checks that your own words ask for it. When they do not, nothing happens and the row beside the Voice button says so, often with a phrasing that works: "nothing in that asks to …, so nothing was done". Names on screen, such as agent labels, folder names and orchestration titles, still cannot ask for an action in your place: the app acts only when it judges that your own words ask for it, and the check that decides is given your words and never those names. Actions that cannot be taken back, or that would lose what is on screen, need the request said on its own: sending the agent's prompt, discarding the New agent form, the typing-mode and reading switches, silencing the app's speech, interrupting the agent, clearing its prompt and scratching the last dictation. A sentence that also says something else, such as "send it, the meeting is at noon", is refused with "asking to … needs to be said on its own". To type into an agent, start by asking to type; the rest of the sentence is the text.

The check adds about a second to each command the Commands service works out, sent as a second request to that service with your words and the app's own description of the commands, and no on-screen names. What the app decides on this machine, such as "send it", a number or "reading on", still runs at once. If the Commands service is slow or cannot be reached, the command is refused with "could not check that this asks to …, so nothing was done", and nothing runs.
