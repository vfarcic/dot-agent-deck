## Voice commands understood by meaning

In the desktop app, voice now goes by what you mean rather than by particular words. Previously a command ran only when your sentence contained one of the words the app expected, so "destroy the tester" was refused with "nothing in that asks to stop an agent" even though it plainly asks for that. Now "destroy the tester" and "get rid of the tester agent" open the same Stop confirmation as "stop the tester", "throw away this draft" discards the New agent form, and "narrate future agent updates aloud" turns reading on. The phrases in the docs are examples, not a list to learn; typing mode still acts only on the phrases it lists, because everything else is typed.

Before a command runs, the app checks that your own words ask for it. When the app judges that they do not, nothing happens and the row beside the Voice button says so, often with a phrasing that works: "nothing in that asks to …, so nothing was done". The app checks your request separately from the names on screen, such as agent labels, folder names and orchestration titles: the check that decides is given your words and not those names. Like any reading of what a sentence means, it can be wrong, so check what happened after you speak; stopping an agent or closing an orchestration still waits for you to confirm by hand, and typing into an agent still shows a countdown you can stop. Some actions run only when the app judges that the request was said on its own: sending the agent's prompt, discarding the New agent form, the typing-mode and reading switches, silencing the app's speech, interrupting the agent, clearing its prompt and scratching the last dictation. A sentence that also says something else, such as "send it, the meeting is at noon", is refused with "asking to … needs to be said on its own". To type into an agent, start by asking to type; the rest of the sentence is the text.

The check runs at the same time as the Commands service works out the command, so with the default Commands service it adds little or no wait. It is sent as two more requests to that service with your words, the app's own description of its commands and which screen you are on, and no on-screen names. What the app decides on this machine, such as "send it", a number or "reading on", still runs at once. If the Commands service is slow or cannot be reached, the command is refused with "could not check that this asks to …, so nothing was done", and nothing runs.
