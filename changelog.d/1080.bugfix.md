## A worker that has reported `work-done` is no longer accused of never having answered

The idle-worker detector exists to tell an orchestrator that a delegated worker has gone quiet. It was also reporting workers that had finished: in one run it named the `release` worker as not having responded with `work-done` at 08:50, for a task that worker had reported complete at 07:13 and whose report the orchestrator had already read. Nothing was outstanding and every pane was idle.

The cause was an accounting rule, not the `work-done` signal going missing. The daemon kept one delegation record per worker pane, and a re-delegation to a pane that had not answered yet replaced that record while counting the older one as a debt still owed. A completion then paid down the debt instead of retiring the delegation it actually answered, leaving the newest record armed over work that was already done. It was not one stray message either: the record survived each completion, so the next delegation re-created the debt and the next completion was misfiled again — once per delegation cycle for the rest of the orchestration. One daemon log covering seven weeks shows it happening 80 times across 30 different worker panes.

A completion now retires its worker pane's whole record, older generations included, so a worker that has just answered owes no idle prompt. The detector is unchanged for the case it exists to catch: a worker that is delegated to and does not answer is still reported, once, on its own deadline — including a worker that was re-delegated to, and including the very next delegation to a pane that answered a moment earlier.

The rule this replaces was itself deliberate ([PRD #126](https://github.com/vfarcic/dot-agent-deck/issues/126) review finding 6) and covered the mirror-image case: a completion arriving late, for a task superseded before it landed, must not disarm the newer delegation's watch. That protection is narrowed rather than kept — a worker re-delegated to *before* it answered, which then stalls on the newer task, now goes unreported until the next delegation to that pane. Both failures need the same state, one the orchestrator protocol already forbids; this one is transient and self-correcting where the other was permanent, and it errs toward a missing nudge rather than a false one. A detector that reports finished work as stuck teaches its reader to ignore it — and the same run that produced this false alarm had two genuine silent workers that this detector caught and that were expensive to diagnose without it.

Telling the two apart properly needs a delegation generation on the `work-done` wire, which there is not today.

The TUI↔daemon protocol is untouched: the record lives in daemon memory, no wire type or field changed, and `PROTOCOL_VERSION` stays at 9. What changed is when the daemon composes an idle prompt for its own orchestrator pane, which an older or newer peer on either side of the socket cannot observe.
