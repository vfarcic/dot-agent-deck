## Deterministic timing for the shell-activity late-sample test

`shell_activity_monitor_ignores_a_pane_that_appeared_after_the_sample_started` timed its whole scenario with fixed sleeps against the shell-activity monitor's 500ms tick — a 700ms sleep guessing that the first tick had already started its sample, and a 2.8s sleep guessing that a later tick had already applied the answer. Two of its three timings left about half a second of slack, so a loaded machine could turn it red in either direction: too early and the new pane became a candidate the sample was entitled to promote, too late and the answer was discarded as stale and the test passed for the wrong reason. It runs in the required `build` and `build-macos` jobs.

It is now sequenced on observable events instead. The stub sampler signals over a channel each time the monitor starts a sample, and the test waits for the first signal before spawning the second pane and for the second signal before reading the statuses — the tick that resumes a retained sample starts none of its own, so that second signal can only come from the tick after the one that collected the late answer. The test also runs on tokio's paused clock, which turns the monitor's `MAX_TABLE_AGE` budget into arithmetic (the answer lands exactly 2.5s old against a 3s bound) rather than a wall-clock race no amount of polling in the test could protect. No production timing constant changed, and the test still fails if the monitor's candidate-snapshot guard is removed. Wall clock for the test drops from about 3.5s to a few milliseconds.
