## A deck prompt waits for the text you have not sent yet

The deck writes into panes on its own: a delegated task into a worker, a worker's completion report into the orchestrator, the idle, silent, exited and blocked worker reports, a dispatched unit's result, a scheduled prompt. Each of those ends with an Enter. If you had typed something into that pane and not sent it, that Enter submitted your text and the deck's as one message, such as `half a thoughtRead .dot-agent-deck/worker-task-coder.md for your task.`

The deck's prompt now waits while the pane holds a draft you typed through the deck and have not sent. Press Enter to send your draft, or Ctrl+U or Ctrl+C to clear it, and the waiting prompt follows as a message of its own. The rest of the deck keeps running while it waits. The wait is capped at 60 seconds by default, so an unattended orchestration cannot stall on a stray keystroke. At the cap the prompt is delivered as it was before, so your draft may be sent together with it; the pane's session is marked Error, which its card shows without the reason, and the daemon's log records why when the daemon was started with `DOT_AGENT_DECK_LOG` set. Set `DOT_AGENT_DECK_DRAFT_DEFER_CAP_MS` on the process that starts the deck to change the cap in milliseconds, or to `0` to switch the wait off; the daemon reads it when it starts.

The deck judges this from the keys it forwards, not from what the agent shows. Text the agent puts in its own input box, such as a prompt recalled from history, is not seen as a draft. A prompt that the TUI or the desktop app sends on your behalf, such as a new orchestration's first prompt to its orchestrator, does not wait. See [A deck prompt waits while you have an unsent draft](https://agent-deck.devopstoolkit.ai/docs/orchestration#a-deck-prompt-waits-while-you-have-an-unsent-draft).
