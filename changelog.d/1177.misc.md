## `issue-queue` chooses the unit shape itself, and takes a total plus a parallelism instead of one batch size

Two changes to how the `issue-queue` skill starts and governs a batch. Contributor tooling only — no product behaviour moves, and nothing under `src/` is touched.

**The shape is now chosen from criteria rather than asked for.** Step 7 used to say the shape was "not deducible from the issue's size, labels or wording. Ask — never infer", which in a sustained loop is one prompt per unit for a question the skill has enough information to answer. The criteria that replace it turn on **divisibility, not size**, and that distinction is the whole point: asking "how big is this?" produces teams on hard problems that do not divide. `--single` covers a change confined to one function, file or tightly-coupled pair; one decision to argue plus its implementation; and a mechanical sweep across many call sites, where splitting the work is what makes the sites inconsistent. `--orchestration` is for separate modules that progress independently, or a design decision plus implementation plus its own verification, or a PRD. **Where the two readings are close the rule is to take `--single`, because the failure modes are not symmetric** — a team in one file produces internal conflicts and a longer path to the same diff, while a single agent on a divisible issue merely takes longer.

**The measurement behind the old rule is kept and re-pointed rather than discarded.** The 2026-08-24 batch, where #669 (a ten-line `lstat` guard) and #668 (an audit plus a reaping mechanism plus its coverage) wanted different shapes, proves that the shape genuinely varies *between issues*. So the half that survives is **per unit, never once for the batch**: applying one shape across a batch is wrong whether it comes from a runner's single answer or from the model's own shortcut. What changed is who decides, not that the decision varies.

**Step 5 now asks two numbers.** A **total** — how many issues to work through altogether, a scope decision belonging to the runner — and a **parallelism**, how many may run at once, which keeps the 2–3 recommendation because that is the number with a machine cost behind it. The skill then runs as a sustained loop, topping up as each unit completes, and keeps a ledger: the loop spans many turns and the dispatched count is not recoverable from the worktree list once finished worktrees are reclaimed.

**One measurement the skill did not have is folded in: a finished unit's worktree is NOT reclaimed automatically**, so `df -h /` has to be read before *each* dispatch rather than once. Measured 2026-09-19 on a 914G disk during a 13-unit loop at parallelism 3 — **46G free (95%)**, with **292G held by five finished units' `target/` trees** while only three units were actually running. That is CLAUDE.md rule 14's territory, where the failure surfaces as a misleading `linking with 'cc' failed` or a `SIGKILL` on `rustc` and the unit that hits it blames its own issue rather than the batch size. `git worktree remove` on a finished unit whose branch is pushed reclaims the space and keeps the branch, so its PR is unaffected.

**The conflict with the product's own contract is scoped, not overridden.** `DISPATCHER_SEED_PROMPT` (`src/ui.rs`) and `docs/dispatcher-mode.md` carry an "ask, do not guess" rule for the shape, and both are left exactly as they are. They govern a bare `dispatch` with no skill in front of it, where there are no criteria and no queue; inside this skill the criteria supply what the ask was for. The narrowing is written down at the point of conflict so it is not read as a general licence to guess when dispatching outside the skill.
