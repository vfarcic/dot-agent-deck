# Doorbell admission

Maintainer reference for how the deck decides whether it may type the doorbell line into an agent's pane, and what it reads to decide. User behaviour belongs in [Orchestration](../orchestration.md#delivery-in-the-tui-and-desktop-app) and [Troubleshooting](../troubleshooting.md); the messaging model around it is in [Agent messaging](agent-messaging.md).

## What the doorbell does, in order

The tick (`src/doorbell.rs`) decides that a ring is due; the ring itself is one transaction on the pane's writer (`AgentPtyRegistry::ring_doorbell` in `src/agent_pty_doorbell.rs`, driving the pure `src/doorbell_txn.rs`). The transaction runs as a supervised task that owns the writer's guard, so a dropped caller does not end it, and it works to one deadline, `D`, `TXN_MAX` (3 s) from the moment the ring has the writer. Admission spends the same budget. While the writer is held, the transaction does not wait on the bus state, the mode store, the ledger, the pane input state or the inbox hub, whose holders can wait on other locks or parse input: the first four are taken by `try_lock`, retried until `D` (the pane input state's holders parse every pane's input bytes), and the tick's admission check reads the inbox hub with `InboxHub::try_doorbell_facts` (a non-blocking `RankedMutex::try_lock_now`; the hub's holders can wait on the registry's record table, whose holders can wait on a generation's bus or mode lock), retrying until `D` as well. Every await while the writer is held waits at most until `D`; a check still unanswered at `D` is no answer, and the deadline ends the attempt: with nothing written before the first byte, suspended after it. Some leaf locks are still taken by waiting under the writer, for example the ring-evidence channel map, the map of transactions in flight and the tripwire list. Their other holders do a map or list operation, a broadcast send or a log line under them, but their hold times are not measured, and `D` does not bound a wait on them. The tripwire fan-out after a dialog takes the registry's record table, so it runs once the writer is released.

1. **The tick.** A generation is considered once its own hooks have reported (`ReadinessLevel` above `Unproven`): a predecessor's Idle, or a shell sample, never readies a replacement. One exception, declared per agent rather than coded into the tick (Q28): an agent whose first own hook comes with its first prompt (`AgentSpec::first_hook` = `FirstHook::WithFirstPrompt`, Codex, measured on 0.164.0 and 0.160.0) reports nothing until someone types to it, so a fresh worker would never be rung. Such a generation is considered before it reports while its own latched release is activated (`Go` or `Inherited`), and every ring's transaction then needs the same generation-specific proof it needs from any generation: its own latched header, a Clean frame of that release's profile showing the empty ready composer, its own mode evidence and its settle anchor. The proof does not raise its `ReadinessLevel` and assumes no own hook route (`hook_route` stays false), so a delivery's submit evidence is the transcript's until its own hooks report. A replacement gets nothing from its predecessor: its latch, evidence, mode store and anchor are its own. It is held, with nothing typed, while its activation refuses it (`cannot_type` on the card after the stall window), while its first ring waits out a settle window, while its doorbell is suspended on an attempt, after an Enter it did not take until its next turn, and while a paste is open. The draft bit (`DraftTracker`) does not hold an activated generation; what the input box holds is read at admission instead, from the screen and from the input model below. The schedule restarts when the queue empties, or when none of the messages the last ring was decided for is still queued (`Bell::covered`), since a ring is confirmed only once the agent took it, which can be after the agent fetched every message it named.
2. **Admission**, with nothing written: the cell read again for a suspension now that the writer is held, so a ring queued behind one that suspended it is not admitted; the generation's activation and screen profile (`doorbell_typing`), its input-mode evidence, the ingress ledger idle (the writer's queue drained, then the outstanding set empty in the same ledger snapshot that records the watermark and job mark as `W0`/`J0`, so a human ticket admitted while the ring waited for the queue refuses it), a Clean evidence frame showing the profile's empty ready composer, read together with the input model ([The input box](#the-input-box)): its placeholder while the model does not say the box holds something, or no text, nothing hidden in the composer's region and the cursor where an empty composer keeps it, only while the model proves the box empty, no repaint nudge in flight, the settle window passed, a submit route (the prompt hook or a measured transcript echo), and the tick's admission still holding. An admitted attempt takes the next attempt number, a fresh token and the latch cell's `Open(a)`.
3. **The line**, `(token) dot-agent-deck: message <id> waiting, run: dot-agent-deck inbox fetch`, enqueued as one deck job while the seam (`DaemonSeam`) holds the ledger, the bus state and the mode store by `try_lock`. The first seam reads the input model again, by `try_lock`, and needs the same empty composer admission did. The token is six letters of `TOKEN_ALPHABET`, redrawn when the candidate is on the admission screen or among the last `RING_SLOTS` attempts. **A staged line** (see below) goes in as two jobs instead: the token head `(xxxxxx)` alone, then the rest once the composer holds exactly the head with the cursor after it, within `ECHO_WAIT`. The rest is queued at a second seam that reads the frame again, synchronously, and requires no dialog and the head still held; a head that never echoes, echoes another token, or shows a dialog gets nothing more. A cancellation while the head's echo is awaited (a lost admission) withdraws the head instead of typing the rest, where the profile can delete and confirm the placeholder.
4. **The echo proof:** the composer shows exactly the line, within `ECHO_WAIT`. A composer that never does ends the attempt `Unresolved(echo_mismatch)`, after deleting the deck's text where the strict recogniser (`Txn::strict`) still finds exactly it.
5. **The pre-Enter recheck**, under the seam, with nothing between its verdict and enqueuing the Enter: the same frame, the job mark still `J0`, the mode still usable, the admission still holding. A human frame that arrived after the line is queued behind the writer and does not stop the Enter; it is written after the release.
6. **Submit evidence**, within `SUBMIT_CONFIRM_WAIT`: this attempt's token reported exact by the prompt hook (the hook arm moves the latch cell on receipt), or the profile's transcript showing the line as a user message while the composer no longer holds it. An empty composer alone proves nothing.
7. **Release.** `Submitted`; `Withdrawn` (deletes sent, the placeholder confirmed back), with `cr_reached` when the Enter had gone, which the tick holds as `not_taken` until the agent's next turn; or `Unresolved(cause)`, which suspends the latch cell before the writer is released and pauses for late evidence until the deadline. A suspended cell holds every later ring until the user submits or clears, or exact evidence for that attempt arrives late.

**Staging (Q27).** The line is staged when a byte after its first stage commits in one of the release's measured dialogs (`exact_verdict`). The first stage is the token head alone: its bytes, the parentheses and `TOKEN_ALPHABET`, are checked against every dialog of the record like any other key, so a record in which one of them commits is `first_stage_commits`, and `inbox/doorbell/053` fails for a recorded table in which one of them is anything but explicitly inert. Everything after the head, the fixed text `dot-agent-deck: message ` included, is the second stage. A staged record also needs `head_echo` verified: the head typed alone, its exact echo in the composer, the rest typed after it, submitted and withdrawn, measured the way the transaction does it (no quiet period inside a measured interval, the Enter at `SUBMIT_DELAY` after the second write). Codex 0.164.0 and 0.160.0 stage (a message-id digit commits in their update and hooks dialogs, `n` in folder trust); Claude Code 2.1.310's dialog table is empty, so its line is one job and needs no `head_echo`.

Each started job has a 200 ms write cap (`PHASE_WRITE_CAP`); a job that has not finished by then is reported to the transaction as stalled and stays the transaction's until it ends. A line job's end counts its accepted bytes as written whatever order the driver saw its reports in, so an end first seen at or after `D`, with no start reported, suspends the attempt rather than calling it nothing written. The transaction asks the tick's admission again every 50 ms while it runs. Lost after the line and before the Enter, with the echo proof holding, it withdraws the line where the profile can delete it and confirm the placeholder, and submits it where the profile has no delete key (the fetch the line asks for then finds nothing); otherwise the attempt is `Unresolved`.

The e2e build adds interleave seams in this order (`tests/support/inbox_doorbell.rs`'s `RING_PHASES`): `decided` (the tick, on a re-ring), `admitted` and `after-line` (the transaction, on a generation's later attempts, the writer held), and `completed`. They are compiled out of every other build.

## The input box

Claude Code 2.1.310 draws nothing for a space typed into its input box: it only moves the cursor (measured on a real session, 2026-10-26), and Codex 0.160.0 and 0.164.0 draw spaces and blank lines the same way, without their placeholder. So three spaces with the cursor moved back to the start, or a line of spaces under another, draw exactly the empty composer, and Claude Code's placeholder is not drawn after a delivered task. The screen cannot prove the box empty there, so admission also reads `ComposerModel` (`src/composer_model.rs`): the input box followed from the bytes the pane's PTY took, the deck's own included, fed with the draft bit in `PaneInputStream`, in the order the PTY took them. It follows them once per measured agent (`Semantics`), and admission asks the one its screen profile names (`ScreenProfile::input`; the e2e stand-in is followed as Claude Code); the card's hold asks the generation's profile through `AgentPtyRegistry::doorbell_input_semantics`, and a pane with no measured profile gets what both followings say together (`InputProof::join`: empty only if both say so).

**What is followed, per agent (Q32).** The model knows the text and the cursor while every key since the last proof of empty is one of these, each measured on that agent: printable ASCII typed, or pasted in a paste of at most `PASTE_FOLLOWED_MAX` (256) characters and three lines (pastes of 800 characters and of three lines went in as text on all three releases; one of 1,200 characters became a placeholder on all three, and one of six lines on Claude Code: `runs/paste-*`); `Ctrl+J` and a line break inside such a paste; Backspace (joins lines at a line's start); Delete (joins the next line at a line break, nothing at the box's end); Left and Right (across line breaks); the Home and End keys (`ESC[H`, `ESC[1~`, `ESC O H` and their End forms) and `Ctrl+A`, `Ctrl+E`; `Ctrl+U`; `Ctrl+C` while no turn is under way; Enter. The agents differ in two places, both measured on 2026-10-26 (`.dot-agent-deck/r5wake-measurements/runs/keyseq-tester-*`, `probe-*`, `probe2-*`, `remedy-*`, gitignored): at a line's start or end that is already where the cursor is, Claude Code's Home and End keys move on to the adjacent line's start or end and its `Ctrl+A`/`Ctrl+E` stay, while Codex's `Ctrl+A`/`Ctrl+E` move on and its Home and End keys stay (`moves_on`); and Enter on a box of blanks keeps them on both, with Claude Code's cursor where it was and Codex's at the start of the box, so text typed next lands before Codex's blanks. On both, `Ctrl+U` deletes from the line's start to the cursor, and at the start of a later line deletes the line break, joining the line to the one above; at the first line's start it does nothing. The model answers `Unknown` after any other key, and in these contexts: Up, Down, non-ASCII text, Alt and modified keys, a longer paste, an Enter after a backslash, `Ctrl+C` during a turn with something in the box (it may only interrupt). Tab, Alt+Enter and keyboard-protocol keys are unknown and may have submitted (Tab submits on Codex), so each owes a possible report (`Key::MaybeSubmit`, `Owed::Maybe`) that a later Enter's confirmation cannot take. An Enter on text holding an `@` (a file menu may complete instead of submitting) or on a leading `!`, `#`, `&`, `/` or `?` with only blanks after it is treated as an Enter on an unknown box. Terminal reports and paste markers edit nothing. The 059 tests replay every measured sequence per agent with the measured contents as the oracle (`MEASURED`, `MEASURED_EDGES`, `MEASURED_END_KILL`), and `doorbell_059_random_sequences_never_read_empty_over_a_held_box` drives random sequences of the measured keys, mixed with unfollowed keys whose effect is picked at random, through the model and a reference editor written from the measurements alone: the model never says `Empty` over a held box, and when it says it knows the box it holds exactly the reference's text and cursor.

`Frame::empty_and_ready` (`src/doorbell_txn.rs`) combines the two: `Empty` and `LineEmpty` admit the placeholder or a blank composer; `Unknown` admits only the placeholder; `Draft` admits nothing, the placeholder included, since the frame may predate the input. The profile's recogniser marks a composer `hidden` when its region holds a row of nothing above text or several rows of nothing (a line break, or a line of spaces); a hidden composer is never empty and never the deck's exact line. Blanks written as cells on the prompt row are not hidden: after a repaint Claude Code writes the spaces a box holds as cells, and clearing them writes blanks over them (measured on 2.1.310, 2026-10-26), so the screen cannot tell them from an empty box and the input model decides. `Unknown` becomes known again on `Ctrl+C` while no turn is under way (it empties every box measured, text, blanks and line breaks, on all three releases; on an empty box Claude Code asks for a second `Ctrl+C` to exit and Codex exits, which is why the user remedy is a letter and then `Ctrl+C`); on `End` then `Ctrl+U` with nothing between on a box known to be one line (on an empty later line `Ctrl+U` joins the line above, so on several lines it does not empty the box: measured); on Claude Code, on a box that may have several lines, `End` then `Ctrl+U` proves only the cursor's line empty with the cursor at its start (`InputProof::LineEmpty`), and `empty_and_ready` admits that with the placeholder or a blank composer, which Claude Code draws only for one empty row: it draws every line as a row and its recogniser marks a blank row among others hidden, and a join leaves the cursor after the text of the line above, off the start (a key that reaches past what was typed since gives it up: Backspace, Left or `Ctrl+U` at its start, Right or Delete at its end, the Home or End key moving on); Codex's recogniser stops at the first blank row, so there it stays `Unknown`, and Codex's placeholder covers the empty box; and on an Enter that the agent's own prompt hook then reports as a prompt it took (Q30). A turn starting is not that evidence: an Enter on a box of blanks submits nothing (measured), and a turn already under way, one the agent starts for another reason, or a late report of an earlier one would confirm it all the same. The daemon hands the model each prompt report from the pane's live generation (`AgentPtyRegistry::note_prompt_report`: a Thinking carrying the hook's `user_prompt`, or the deck's own line the hook recognised, `PromptReport::Deck`), and the model keeps, oldest first, what each submit it counted still owes: a box it knew was submitted owes a report carrying its text (`Owed::Prompt`), and an unknown Enter it settled some other way before its report (by `Ctrl+C`, `End` then `Ctrl+U`, a prompt sent, or giving up), or a key that may have submitted, owes one that may come (`Owed::Maybe`, forgotten once the agent has ended the turn the report would come in, or the turn after when a turn or another owed report was already under way). A report goes to the oldest entry that can own it, a known box's report settling every older entry, since the agent takes its prompts in order; only a report none of them can own confirms the unconfirmed Enter. Until then, what is typed after that Enter is followed as typed into an empty box, and a key that reaches past it into what the Enter may have left (Left, Backspace or `Ctrl+U` at its start; Home on its first two lines, since a Home key can move on to the line above; Right or Delete at its end; End on its last two lines; an Enter at the start of text typed after it) gives the Enter up: the box is `Unknown`, its lines counted from both. Enter on text typed after it submits the whole box. While an escape sequence is still arriving the model answers `Unknown`, except after a lone ESC, which swallows nothing; an OSC or DCS that does not open with a number, an over-long one, or one with a control key inside is an unfollowed key, and the byte that showed it is read afresh, so `End` then `Ctrl+U` still recovers a box of one line. The card's `possible_draft` reason reads the draft bit or a model that does not prove the box empty.

**Replaying a byte log.** `src/composer_model.rs` compiles on its own (its tests aside), so a small program can include it by path and replay a captured input stream: `#[path = "<checkout>/src/composer_model.rs"] mod composer_model;`, then for each byte `model.feed_byte(byte, submits, in_paste, false)` and `model.proof(Some(composer_model::Semantics::Codex))` (or `ClaudeCode`), with `{model:?}` showing each agent's text and cursor. `submits` is an Enter outside a paste (`\r` not after an ESC), and `in_paste` the bracketed-paste framing, as `PaneInputStream` decides them.

## Accepted residuals

The checks narrow what the deck can type into; they do not close every window. These are accepted, and the user pages promise only what the deck detects:

- **A dialog after the final check.** The pre-Enter recheck reads the frame the evidence state last parsed, then the Enter is queued under the same seam. A dialog the agent opens after that read and before its input loop takes the Enter receives the Enter. Nothing on the deck's side can see that frame in time.
- **A dialog after the second stage's seam.** On a staged line, the rest is queued at a seam that reads the frame again, synchronously, right before the enqueue; that seam is the existing staged-typing check, not something the token-head boundary added. A dialog the agent opens after that read and before it takes the rest receives the rest: the fixed text, whose `n` leaves Codex's folder-trust dialog for another screen and whose letters include keys that move a highlight, and the id's digits, of which `1`, `2` and `3` commit in Codex's update and hooks dialogs. A screen reached that way can receive the remaining bytes before the deck sees it. Compared with a first stage of the token and the fixed text, the token-head boundary makes the first stage eight measured-inert bytes and the second stage longer; it is the same kind of race, and nothing measured shows the overall exposure is smaller. The first ring's settle window, from the slowest measured start-up dialog (6.03 s on Codex 0.164.0, 6.69 s on 0.160.0, at least 10 s on an inherited release), keeps rings clear of the start-up dialogs those measurements saw; it does not cover a dialog that opens later, or an inherited release whose dialogs come later than the measured ones. A dialog that opens after the first stage's seam receives only the head, whose bytes are inert in every measured dialog, and the head's missing echo then stops the rest.
- **What the input model cannot see.** Text the agent puts into its own box (a prompt restored after a blocked submit) is invisible to it; a key that reaches a dialog or menu instead of the box is read as an edit of the box, so an Enter on text or a `Ctrl+C` that a dialog took reads as emptying it; and a prompt the agent submits by itself, not from its box, reported while an unknown Enter that left blanks awaits its report, confirms that Enter. A report of an earlier submit whose text does not carry what the model followed (the agent rewriting a prompt before its hook sees it) can do the same. In each, a box holding only blanks can read as empty. A frame read just after a key and before the agent has redrawn for it can also show an older composer; the model is in the PTY's order, the screen is not.
- **An Enter left unconfirmed.** The other way round, the model holds: a submit the agent never reports (a command it handles without its prompt hook) leaves its entry owed until a later report carrying another prompt's text settles it; a report that arrives before its own Enter was recorded, an unknown Enter settled before its report and followed by another before a turn ends, or a report a containment match gives to the wrong known box, each leaves an unknown Enter unconfirmed. The card shows `possible_draft`, and `Ctrl+C`, `End` then `Ctrl+U` on a box of one line, or a prompt typed and sent, recovers it; the user pages name a letter then `Ctrl+C`, which empties every measured box without sending anything.
- **Between mode samples.** A wrapped pane's input mode is a sample the wrapper took at some moment before the check, refused once it is older than `MODE_MAX_AGE_NS`. A change to the terminal's mode after the newest accepted sample and before the deck's bytes reach the agent is not seen; the transaction stops on a change only once a sample shows it (`ModeChanged`). An unwrapped pane reads the master's termios at each check, so its window is the time between that read and the write.

## Activation, and what the card and WAKE say

`generation_activation` (`src/doorbell_generation.rs`) decides per generation, from its effective agent type and the release latched from its start-up header:

- no effective agent type, or a type no record measures (OpenCode, Pi, Devin today): `unmeasured_release`, whatever the start-up screen showed;
- then the table in `src/doorbell_activation.rs`, rule by rule: the latch (`unknown_release`, `release_conflict`, `scanner_geometry`, `scanner_budget`), an exact record (complete → `Go`, otherwise its own NoGo), Codex-only inheritance from the newest older complete record within `INHERIT_MINOR_SPAN` minors, the tripwire, and the inherited settle window against `SETTLE_CAP`.

On the records this branch ships, Claude Code 2.1.310 and 2.1.312 are `Go` and both Codex records stage the line and are `missing_measurement` until their `head_echo` is recorded, so nothing inherits: the deck types into those two Claude Code releases and into no other agent. A Claude Code release with no record of its own is `unmeasured_release` (Q15: no Claude inheritance). That is the cost the user docs state: after Claude Code updates itself, the doorbell stays off for it until a deck release measures the new version, and `inbox/claude/014` lapses with it (Q17): it reports `SKIP:` naming the installed and the measured releases, which `DOT_AGENT_DECK_REQUIRE_REAL_E2E=1` turns into a failure.

The verdict reaches the card, WAKE and the overdue notice through the re-projection publisher, never from admission directly:

- **One writer.** `reproject` reads the generation's latch cell (`uncertain_input`, `dialog_during_ring`) and its refusal: the activation clause, then the clauses that hold it for the rest of its life (Q18: `input_mode_conflict`, `clock_namespace`, `clock_namespace_unreadable`, `attempt_exhausted`). Transient causes (a stale or cooked mode sample, `input_mode_unverified`, a Dirty frame, the settle window) stay out, so WAKE does not flap while a ring is on its way. Runs are serialised by one mutex, `doorbell_publishing`, shared by the publisher task and a spawn's own run.
- **When it runs.** At spawn, synchronously, before the spawn returns (unlatched, so `unknown_release` until the header is read); then on request: a release latched or changed, the agent type learned from hooks, an `Unresolved` attempt (the transaction waits up to 100 ms for that run), a sticky mode clause first holding the generation, its last attempt number spent, and the cell moving on user or hook evidence. Requests for one generation queued while it waits coalesce; one arriving during its run is queued again behind it. Each run logs `queued_us` and `took_us`, and a run that panics is logged and the publisher goes on.
- **The fence.** The hub write (`InboxHub::set_wake_projection`) happens only while the generation is still the pane's live one, asked under the hub's lock. A generation's exit marks it no longer current before its hub entries go, so a run that read its inputs before an exit and writes after it puts nothing back for the retired generation.
- **What reads it.** `wake_of` picks the route for both the snapshot's WAKE and the overdue notice: an armed waiter → `native`, a turn-end announcement → `turn-end`, a refused typed fallback → `none`, otherwise `doorbell`; the record boundary keeps `none` for a record with no live status. The copy can lag admission by the publisher's latency, in either direction; admission stays the authority, so a stale label never permits or stops typing. A notice already posted keeps the route it was composed with.
- **The log.** When a generation's refusal changes, `doorbell off: the deck does not type into this agent` names the clause and the releases of its agent the deck has measured; the user troubleshooting page points at it.

## Input-mode evidence

An unwrapped pane's agent reads its input from the deck's PTY slave, so the master's termios (a duplicated descriptor held by `GenerationDoorbell`) is its input mode: raw, no echo, or the doorbell types nothing. A wrapped pane's evidence is what the wrapper reports (`src/input_mode.rs`): each sample carries two monotonic stamps, from before and after its `tcgetattr`, refused when they are wider than `MODE_MAX_AGE_NS` apart and aged from the first; a report is accepted only from a process in the session the pane's child leads, bound to the first reporter's `(pid, start time)` (a second one is `input_mode_conflict` for the generation's life), on the daemon's time namespace; the wrapper sends reports only to a daemon identity that answered its capability probe with `input_mode_report`, caching that verdict per identity. An unwrapped pane that a process reports for anyway is read from the store from then on, so a conflict holds it there too.

## The e2e-only seams

- **The stand-in profile.** `STANDIN_PROFILE` (`src/doorbell_screen.rs`) recognises the fixed rows the test stand-ins draw. It is selected by `DOT_AGENT_DECK_TEST_COMPOSER_PROFILE=standin` with `DOT_AGENT_DECK_TEST_COMPOSER_BINARY` naming an absolute path, from the start request's environment or an assignment in its command, the command naming that path; or from the daemon's own environment, for agents the TUI spawns from a configured command a test cannot write. Either way it is typed into only while the pane's process runs that path (`standin_running`, read from `/proc`, so Linux only). None of it is compiled outside the `e2e` feature.
- **The record exclusion.** `DOT_AGENT_DECK_E2E_UNRECORD_CLAUDE_RELEASES` removes Claude Code records by bare release, so `inbox/claude/050` can run a real agent on a release the deck treats as unmeasured. It names Claude Code releases only: with no Claude inheritance (rule 3), removing a Claude record can only turn that release's `Go` into `unmeasured_release`, while a general filter could have removed a Codex record that another release's inheritance or settle window is read from. A malformed list is refused whole. The production build has a stub that returns an empty list and reads no variable.

## The parsers' work budgets

Two `vt100` parsers run inside the PTY reader's push, under the pane's output lock (`AgentBus::push` in `src/agent_pty.rs`): the release scanner (`src/release_scanner.rs`), which reads a generation's release off its start-up header, and the evidence parser (`src/evidence_state.rs`), which reads the agent's composer. Whatever they cost is time every subscriber of that pane, its resizes and its evidence reads wait for. Most output costs them a few dozen nanoseconds a byte. Some `vt100` operations work over the whole grid, so a pane at 512 × 512 that prints erase-display without pause costs each erase 262 144 cell clears: measured before the budgets, 600 ms of scanner CPU over a 256 KiB window and 19.9 ms of added lock hold per push for the scanner, 18.3 ms for the evidence parser.

### What is counted

Both parsers are fed through `src/vt_work.rs`'s `MeteredParser`, one byte at a time. Before a byte reaches `vt100`, a shadow `vte` tokenizer (the one `vt100` itself runs on, checked by `the_meter_and_vt100_share_one_vte` to be a single version in `Cargo.lock`) prices it in **work units**: 64 for every byte's own parse and check, plus one unit for each cell the operation it completes can write or move. The prices are upper bounds read from `vt100` 0.18.1's `screen.rs`, `grid.rs` and `row.rs`; the module documentation lists them operation by operation. A byte whose price would take the caller past its limit is **not parsed**, and nothing after it is.

The count is of work, not of time, so a loaded machine cannot make a normal launch look expensive, and the bound holds by construction: no push gives a parser more than its limit's worth of units, however the bytes were split, because the check runs before each byte and not after the push.

What a unit costs in time is measured, not derived. `scanner_budget_calibration` in `src/agent_pty_scanner_budget.rs` prices every recorded launch and fifteen pathological traces with no limit and reports their CPU time per unit; `scanner_budget_measurement` replays them through the real push path. On the measuring machine (16 cores, release profile, 2026-10-25) the dearest figures were 0.44 to 0.57 ns per unit for a short recorded launch, varying run to run, 0.43 for the dearest pathological trace priced to its end, and 0.57 for a push that trips a limit through the push path, where a fresh grid's page faults and the push's own work fall inside the same 5 million units. The first pricing runs found three operations priced low, and each was repriced before the limits were set: a full reset (`ESC c`, 2.9 ns per unit, from reallocating every row) and the first switch to the alternate screen (which allocates its grid) now cost `16 × R × C`, and erase-in-line (1.2 ns, erased cell by cell) `4 × C`.

### The limits

| parser | limit | units | at 0.57 ns per unit | the most a recorded launch uses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| release scanner | per push (`SCANNER_WORK_PER_PUSH`) | 5 000 000 | 2.9 ms | 559 680 |
| release scanner | per generation (`SCANNER_WORK_TOTAL`) | 85 000 000 | 48 ms | 12 643 784; a whole 256 KiB window of launches, 19 963 226 (7.8 ms of CPU) |
| evidence parser | per push (`EVIDENCE_WORK_PER_PUSH`) | 5 000 000 | 2.9 ms | none reaches it (`doorbell_044_recorded_launches_stay_within_the_evidence_bound`) |

On the measuring machine the two per-push limits together kept a push's added lock hold under the 10 ms maximum plan §4.6 sets, with room for a machine somewhat slower than that one. The work bound is structural; the time it takes is not (see [What the budgets do not claim](#what-the-budgets-do-not-claim)). The evidence parser has no per-generation limit: it has no byte window and runs for the generation's life, so only its per-push cost is bounded.

A normal launch reaches the per-push limit only in a pane near the cell budget: a first switch to the alternate screen at 512 × 512 costs 4.5 million units on its own, so the read that carries it and a redraw can pass 5 million. That is the case the `scanner_budget` clause tells a user about (make the window smaller and start the agent afresh).

### What a trip does

- **Release scanner.** The scanner is dropped and its window ends (`pane release` says `scanner dropped: work over budget`, and the `release scanner timing` line names the limit, `push` or `total`, beside `work` and `push_work_max`). A generation with no release latched yet reads as `scanner_budget`, which the activation table holds with that clause; a release already latched stays latched.
- **Evidence parser.** The parser is dropped and the state is `WorkOverBudget` (`work_over_budget` in the `pane layout` line), which recognises nothing, so nothing admitted on a Clean frame can be. The next layout edge, a resize or a repaint nudge, starts the evidence over, exactly as after any edge.

Resizes are not charged to either budget. A resize is the deck's own ioctl, not the agent's output, and its cost is recorded separately (`resize_us_max`; up to 6.4 ms measured, to 512 × 512 among other sizes).

### Measured with the budgets in

Plan §9.1's harness, `scanner_budget_measurement`, replayed every recorded launch through the real push path at its recorded read sizes, with and without each parser, on the measuring machine (15 runs; p99 is the nearest rank over every push of every run):

| trace | added lock hold per push | scanner CPU per generation | outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 16 recorded launches at 50 × 200 and 80 × 300 (129 900 pushes) | p99 69 µs, max 501 µs | at most 5.0 ms | no limit reached (the fast tests below) |
| a whole 256 KiB window of recorded launches, 50 × 200 and 80 × 300 | p99 43 µs, max 447 µs | at most 7.8 ms | no limit reached |
| eight panes at once, 50 × 200 (31 116 pushes) | p99 88 µs, max 963 µs | — | no limit reached |
| evidence parser, every recorded launch | p99 349 µs, max 484 µs | — | no limit reached |
| the fifteen pathological traces, scanner | at most 2.9 ms | at most 33.9 ms | dropped at a limit, or, for delete and insert characters, inside both to the byte limit (10.7 ms) |
| the fifteen pathological traces, evidence parser | at most 2.7 ms | — | dropped at the per-push limit where a read reaches it |

The pathological traces are erase-display with header-like rows at 512 × 512, line feeds at the bottom and reverse index at the top, scroll-up and insert-line by 4096, a 65 535-character repeat, eight alternate-screen switches in one sequence, full reset, erase-in-line twice (cursor anywhere and at the row's start), erase, delete and insert characters, erase below the cursor, and prints at the row end, each at the geometry within the cell budget that makes it dearest. Before the budgets, the first of them cost the scanner 600 ms of CPU and 19.9 ms per push. `doorbell_055_recorded_launches_stay_within_the_scanner_budget` and `doorbell_044_recorded_launches_stay_within_the_evidence_bound` keep the recorded launches inside the limits in `cargo test-fast`, and `doorbell_055_the_scanner_works_to_a_budget` and `doorbell_044_the_evidence_parser_works_to_a_per_push_bound` pin what a trip does.

### What the budgets do not claim

- The time figures are units times the cost per unit measured on one machine. A machine half as fast spends twice the time on the same units; the work bound is the same everywhere.
- A pane whose every push stays under the evidence parser's per-push limit costs that much on every push for as long as it prints. The bound is per push, as decided, not a rate limit.
- The prices are upper bounds read from `vt100` 0.18.1. A `vt100` update that adds an operation over many cells needs a price for it, and the calibration rerun.
