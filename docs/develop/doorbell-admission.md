# Doorbell admission

Maintainer reference for how the deck decides whether it may type the doorbell line into an agent's pane, and what it reads to decide. User behaviour belongs in [Orchestration](../orchestration.md#delivery-in-the-tui-and-desktop-app) and [Troubleshooting](../troubleshooting.md); the messaging model around it is in [Agent messaging](agent-messaging.md).

## What the doorbell does, in order

The tick (`src/doorbell.rs`) decides that a ring is due; the ring itself is one transaction on the pane's writer (`AgentPtyRegistry::ring_doorbell` in `src/agent_pty_doorbell.rs`, driving the pure `src/doorbell_txn.rs`). The transaction runs as a supervised task that owns the writer's guard, so a dropped caller does not end it, and it works to one deadline, `D`, `TXN_MAX` (3 s) from the moment the ring has the writer. Admission spends the same budget. While the writer is held, the transaction does not wait on the bus state, the mode store, the ledger, the pane input state or the inbox hub, whose holders can wait on other locks or parse input: the first four are taken by `try_lock`, retried until `D` (the pane input state's holders parse every pane's input bytes), and the tick's admission check reads the inbox hub with `InboxHub::try_doorbell_facts` (a non-blocking `RankedMutex::try_lock_now`; the hub's holders can wait on the registry's record table, whose holders can wait on a generation's bus or mode lock), retrying until `D` as well. Every await while the writer is held waits at most until `D`; a check still unanswered at `D` is no answer, and the deadline ends the attempt: with nothing written before the first byte, suspended after it. Some leaf locks are still taken by waiting under the writer, for example the ring-evidence channel map and the map of transactions in flight. Their other holders do a map operation, a broadcast send or a log line under them, but their hold times are not measured, and `D` does not bound a wait on them.

1. **The tick.** A generation is considered once its own hooks have reported (`ReadinessLevel` above `Unproven`): a predecessor's Idle, or a shell sample, never readies a replacement. One exception, declared per agent rather than coded into the tick (Q28): an agent whose first own hook comes with its first prompt (`AgentSpec::first_hook` = `FirstHook::WithFirstPrompt`, Codex, measured on 0.164.0 and 0.160.0) reports nothing until someone types to it, so a fresh worker would never be rung. Such a generation is considered before it reports while its own latched release is activated (`Go`), and every ring's transaction then needs the same generation-specific proof it needs from any generation: its own latched header, a Clean frame of that release's profile showing the empty ready composer, its own mode evidence and its settle anchor. The proof does not raise its `ReadinessLevel` and assumes no own hook route (`hook_route` stays false), so a delivery's submit evidence is the transcript's until its own hooks report. A replacement gets nothing from its predecessor: its latch, evidence, mode store and anchor are its own. It is held, with nothing typed, while its activation refuses it (`cannot_type` on the card after the stall window), while its first ring waits out a settle window, while its doorbell is suspended on an attempt, after an Enter it did not take until its next turn, and while a paste is open. Whether the input box is empty is read at admission, from the screen and from the generation's input epoch (below). The schedule restarts when the queue empties, or when none of the messages the last ring was decided for is still queued (`Bell::covered`), since a ring is confirmed only once the agent took it, which can be after the agent fetched every message it named.
2. **Admission**, with nothing written: the cell read again for a suspension now that the writer is held, so a ring queued behind one that suspended it is not admitted; the generation's activation and screen profile (`doorbell_typing`), its input-mode evidence, the ingress ledger idle (the writer's queue drained, then the outstanding set empty in the same ledger snapshot that records the watermark and job mark as `W0`/`J0`, so a human ticket admitted while the ring waited for the queue refuses it), a Clean evidence frame showing the profile's empty ready composer, read together with the generation's input epoch ([The input box](#the-input-box)): its placeholder, or no text, nothing hidden in the composer's region and the cursor where an empty composer keeps it, and in either case only while the epoch says the box is untouched, no repaint nudge in flight, the settle window passed, a submit route (the prompt hook or a measured transcript echo), and the tick's admission still holding. An admitted attempt takes the next attempt number, a fresh token and the latch cell's `Open(a)`.
3. **The line**, `(token) dot-agent-deck: message <id> waiting, run: dot-agent-deck inbox fetch`, enqueued as one deck job while the seam (`DaemonSeam`) holds the ledger, the bus state and the mode store by `try_lock`. The first seam reads `touched` from the input epoch again, inside the ledger's hold, and needs the same empty, untouched composer admission did. The token is six letters of `TOKEN_ALPHABET`, redrawn when the candidate is on the admission screen or among the last `RING_SLOTS` attempts. **A staged line** (see below) goes in as two jobs instead: the token head `(xxxxxx)` alone, then the rest once the composer holds exactly the head with the cursor after it, within `ECHO_WAIT`. The rest is queued at a second seam that reads the frame again, synchronously, and requires no dialog and the head still held; a head that never echoes, echoes another token, or shows a dialog gets nothing more. A cancellation while the head's echo is awaited (a lost admission) withdraws the head instead of typing the rest, where the profile can delete and confirm the placeholder.
4. **The echo proof:** the composer shows exactly the line, within `ECHO_WAIT`. A composer that never does ends the attempt `Unresolved(echo_mismatch)`, after deleting the deck's text where the strict recogniser (`Txn::strict`) still finds exactly it.
5. **The pre-Enter recheck**, under the seam, with nothing between its verdict and enqueuing the Enter: the same frame, the job mark still `J0`, the mode still usable, the admission still holding. A human frame that arrived after the line is queued behind the writer and does not stop the Enter; it is written after the release.
6. **Submit evidence**, within `SUBMIT_CONFIRM_WAIT`: this attempt's token reported exact by the prompt hook (the hook arm moves the latch cell on receipt), or the profile's transcript showing the line as a user message while the composer no longer holds it. An empty composer alone proves nothing.
7. **Release.** `Submitted`; `Withdrawn` (deletes sent, the placeholder confirmed back), with `cr_reached` when the Enter had gone, which the tick holds as `not_taken` until the agent's next turn; or `Unresolved(cause)`, which suspends the latch cell before the writer is released and pauses for late evidence until the deadline. A suspended cell holds every later ring until the user submits or clears, or exact evidence for that attempt arrives late; lifting it grants nothing the epoch doesn't ([The input box](#the-input-box)).

**Staging (Q27).** The line is staged when a byte after its first stage commits in one of the release's measured dialogs (`exact_verdict`). The first stage is the token head alone: its bytes, the parentheses and `TOKEN_ALPHABET`, are checked against every dialog of the record like any other key, so a record in which one of them commits is `first_stage_commits`, and `inbox/doorbell/053` fails for a recorded table in which one of them is anything but explicitly inert. Everything after the head, the fixed text `dot-agent-deck: message ` included, is the second stage. A staged record also needs `head_echo` verified: the head typed alone, its exact echo in the composer, the rest typed after it, submitted and withdrawn, measured the way the transaction does it (no quiet period inside a measured interval, the Enter at `SUBMIT_DELAY` after the second write). Codex 0.164.0 and 0.160.0 stage (a message-id digit commits in their update and hooks dialogs, `n` in folder trust); Claude Code 2.1.310's dialog table is empty, so its line is one job and needs no `head_echo`.

Each started job has a 200 ms write cap (`PHASE_WRITE_CAP`); a job that has not finished by then is reported to the transaction as stalled and stays the transaction's until it ends. A line job's end counts its accepted bytes as written whatever order the driver saw its reports in, so an end first seen at or after `D`, with no start reported, suspends the attempt rather than calling it nothing written. The transaction asks the tick's admission again every 50 ms while it runs. Lost after the line and before the Enter, with the echo proof holding, it withdraws the line where the profile can delete it and confirm the placeholder, and submits it where the profile has no delete key (the fetch the line asks for then finds nothing); otherwise the attempt is `Unresolved`.

The e2e build adds interleave seams in this order (`tests/support/inbox_doorbell.rs`'s `RING_PHASES`): `decided` (the tick, on a re-ring), `admitted` and `after-line` (the transaction, on a generation's later attempts, the writer held), and `completed`. They are compiled out of every other build.

## The input box

Claude Code 2.1.310 draws nothing for a space typed into its input box: it only moves the cursor (measured on a real session, 2026-10-26), and Codex 0.160.0 and 0.164.0 draw spaces and blank lines the same way, without their placeholder. So three spaces with the cursor moved back to the start, or a line of spaces under another, draw exactly the empty composer, and Claude Code's placeholder is not drawn after a delivered task. The screen cannot prove the box empty there, and since Q35-21 the deck no longer tries to follow the box from the keys: admission reads the generation's **input epoch** (`src/composer_probe/epoch.rs`, R3 §3). Every byte-carrying op the PTY writer accepts moves `epoch`; `observed` is the epoch through which something proved the box empty, and the box is *touched* while they differ (`InputEpoch::touched`). Three things move `observed`: a probe that read the box empty with nothing else entering since its admission (`InputEpoch::apply`, R3 §3.4), the ring's own `Submitted` reconcile (R3 §3.5, below), and its `Withdrawn` reconcile (the Q35-21 ruling, below). No hook moves any of the numbers (`inbox/probe/002`): a prompt report, a turn edge or the agent's own Idle says nothing about what the box holds.

`Frame::empty_and_ready` (`src/doorbell_txn.rs`) admits the placeholder or a blank composer (no text, the cursor where an empty one keeps it, nothing `hidden`) only while `Frame::touched` is false; the admission read, the final seam before the first byte and the settle window's ready-frame read all take `touched` from the epoch in the same hold as the frame. The profile's recogniser marks a composer `hidden` when its region holds a row of nothing above text or several rows of nothing (a line break, or a line of spaces); a hidden composer is never empty and never the deck's exact line.

**The ring's reconcile.** Each of a transaction's jobs is queued as `ByteSource::Ring(DeckJobId::ring(attempt))`, so the epoch records it as that job's op, not as foreign input. At `Submitted`, with the ingress ledger held across the update and its job mark still the transaction's `J0`, `GenerationInput::reconcile_ring` sets `observed = epoch` iff the newest op is the ring's own, `last_foreign <= E_admit` (the epoch read at admission) and the box was untouched at admission. The ledger hold keeps every ticketed person's op out between the check and the update; an external edit (`GenerationInput::external_edit`) can still move the epoch without the ledger, and `last_foreign <= E_admit` refuses the reconcile then. A `Withdrawn` ring reconciles the same way: it ends only once the deck deleted its own line and saw the agent's placeholder again, drawn only over an empty box. Nothing else reconciles a ring: one that wrote nothing moved no number, and an unresolved one leaves the box touched by its own bytes, so the next attempt needs a check.

**The hold reason** (`PaneInputHold::of`, `src/agent_pty.rs`): `draft = touched && permission == Armable`, which the card shows as `possible_draft` (a check is on its way, or the last one read the box non-empty). A touched box whose generation isn't `Armable` is `PaneInputHold::unchecked`, and the tick holds its ring (`Facts::held`) and projects `AgentPtyRegistry::doorbell_unchecked_hold`: `cannot_check` while the permission is `Pending`, `needs_fetch` for a Claude Code generation (no native waiter is armed, or the tick would not be asked), `cannot_type` for every other agent. An `Armable` generation whose schedule is backing off after a failed check (`GenerationInput::probe_retrying`) shows `cannot_check` too, while the tick keeps asking its retry. A generation whose typed doorbell is refused keeps its own `cannot_type` clause, ranked as before. Which generations are `Armable` is the activation's: the Claude activation (Q35-28, PRD #1666 part 3) set `INSTALL_ROLLOUT` (`src/composer_probe/install.rs`) to `Direct(Claude)` and verified Claude Code 2.1.317's and 2.1.318's `editor_probe` (`CLAUDE_2_1_317_EDITOR_PROBE`, `CLAUDE_2_1_318_EDITOR_PROBE`, equal by `doorbell_053_the_measured_2_1_318_editor_record`); a release must carry a verified `editor_probe` record, and 2.1.310's and 2.1.312's stay `Absent`. `Launcher(Claude)` and `Wrapper` can be bound since Q35-22 (the shared discovery below the PTY child, below) but are not in the rollout yet, and Codex's classes wait for Q35-29. Activation is Linux-only: on any other host `install::at_spawn` returns `NotInstalled::Platform` whatever the rollout, and `composer_probe_tick` (`src/agent_pty_segments.rs`) is compiled to `false`, so no generation there has an observer (`permission = None`) and a touched Claude Code box holds `needs_fetch` until restart, exactly like a launcher pane. Bringing the check to macOS needs a macOS observer. From then on, a touched box of a Claude Code generation the deck spawned directly at such a release, on a Linux host, is checked, shown `possible_draft` while a check is outstanding or read it non-empty, and rung once a check reads it empty. A touched box of any other generation is never untouched again: a Claude Code generation the deck would type into (a launcher's until its class is rolled out, a direct one on any non-Linux host, and a direct one at a release without a verified `editor_probe`) shows `needs_fetch` and a Codex one `cannot_type`, until the agent restarts, and every later message holds the same way while the prompt or turn-end route still delivers. `HoldDetail` is not produced yet: `cannot_check` shows its generic words.

**Binding `A` (Q35-22).** A generation is `Armable` only once its agent root `A` is bound. A direct Claude's `A` comes from the deck's own launch record (`bound_agent`, `src/composer_probe/observe.rs`). Every other installed class's comes from the shared discovery (`src/composer_probe/discovery.rs`, Q28(b)'s D1 resolver as Q31 and Q102b made it concrete):
- **Pinned at spawn.** Before the child exists, the spawn path (`Installation::pin_natives`) resolves, on the `PATH` the PTY child gets, a direct pane's program word, a launcher's agent name, and both agents' names for an undeclared wrapper, passing over the deck's telemetry shims and following symlinks to a `(dev, ino)`. The file is opened once, and its identity (`fstat`) and its shebang are both read from that open file. Nothing looks a name up again. For a native agent, whose process is checked by its `/proc/<pid>/exe`, an agent updated after the spawn keeps its old pin, a wrapper that then `exec`s the new inode is refused, and the next spawn pins the new one. A name that resolves nothing refuses `executable not resolved: expected <agent_type>`.
- **Interpreter-hosted entrypoints.** When the pinned file is a script (npm Codex's `#!/usr/bin/env node` `codex.js`), the pin also carries its interpreter, resolved at spawn as the kernel and `env` would, and the argv they give it. A process running that interpreter with that argv (and, below the deck's wrapper, the wrapped arguments at its end) is an attested entrypoint, and `A` is the native agent it spawns as its child. Another script under the same interpreter, or the script under another interpreter, is not one, and a native below it is refused as an executable mismatch. The entrypoint's own executable is the interpreter's, which says nothing about the script it read, so validation also requires the script's path to hold the pinned file still: a script replaced after the pin (an npm update, or any writer of that directory) refuses the native `executable differs`. That read is a refusal only, never a lookup that attests. npm Claude, whose agent is the interpreter process itself, is not bound by this rule yet.
- **What the hosted route leaves open.** Each of these needs a process that can already write the agent's installation as the user: (1) the script's path is read at validation, not when the interpreter opened it, so a file swapped in before the interpreter's read and back before validation isn't seen; (2) a pin is a file identity, not its content, so a write into the pinned file that keeps its inode isn't seen (for a native's pin too); (3) the native below an attested entrypoint is attested by that parentage alone and its own executable is compared with nothing, because where it lives is the script's run-time choice (npm's `codex.js` resolves a platform package, which npm may hoist), so there is no spawn-time fact to pin it to, and a writer of the package's directory can replace it.
- **The walk and its validation.** The walk goes down from the PTY child, breadth first and bounded, and takes the first process whose `argv[0]` or executable is named `claude` or `codex`; below the deck's `wrap` it passes over a child still running the deck's executable and the wrapper's `codex app-server` trust probe. The candidate is held by its pidfd and read again, and refused, in this order: exited; left the parent it was found under, or its ancestry to the PTY child changed; owned by another user; executable neither the pin nor a child of a still-attested entrypoint. The candidate's executable read before the pidfd poll and after it must both be the one validation accepted, and that is the executable `gen-G` publishes, so a candidate that `exec`s anything in between is refused `executable differs`. A candidate the pidfd can't hold for a reason other than its exit (a full descriptor table, say) is refused `candidate not held`, not reported as exited. Each refusal is final for the generation (`Inert(Unbound)`). #2002's `-c shell_environment_policy.set={}` pair is left out of every argv comparison on both sides (A-20d-13).
- **An undeclared wrapper's kind.** Its own hooks' first reported agent (`set_agent_type_for_generation`) is a claim: it selects which pin is expected and attaches that agent's segments, never authority. `kind-G` is written with `gen-G` at `Armable`, and only then does the helper's attestation take the generation for that agent.
- **The `e2e` build's interfaces.** `binding-status` (read-only: permission and refusal reason), `DOT_AGENT_DECK_E2E_DISCOVERY_GATE` (a barrier between finding the candidate and validating it, ended at once by a retirement) and `DOT_AGENT_DECK_E2E_DISCOVERY_REJECT_UID` (forces only the foreign-owner refusal). A release build has none of them. `inbox/probe/035` covers them.

**The startup trust write (loc10's window).** The daemon's Codex startup install writes trust into its `$CODEX_HOME/config.toml` from a thread of its own (issue #1665), and that file is a Codex key-binding source for a pane in that home or started in `$HOME`. A write after a generation's baseline refuses every check of the generation `Keymap(Changed)`. So an installed generation whose class reads Codex's sources waits for that thread, at most `STARTUP_TRUST_WAIT` (6 s), before it reads its baseline (`codex_hooks_manage::await_startup_trust`). The wait comes before the spawn rather than the baseline after it, because the baseline must be read before the child exists (A-20d-1b): a read after the spawn could not tell the startup write from the agent's own. Only installed generations wait, so no spawn outside the rollout is slower. Not covered: a startup trust write from another process (the TUI's own startup install), which this in-process wait cannot see; it still refuses that generation's checks, failing closed.

**What the Claude activation accepts, and the user docs state** (`docs/orchestration.md#how-the-deck-checks-claude-codes-input-box`): (1) under `EC_POLICY = Hold`, one use of the person's own editor (Ctrl+G) leaves the pane's writer scope open, so every later check in that generation is refused `Records(Scope(Open(EditorReturnUnchecked)))`, the card says `cannot_check`, and a queued task waits for the person's next prompt (amendment 6 §1.3, ruling (d); `inbox/probe/042`'s last round and `040`'s one trial per pane pin it, so a change of policy re-oracles both); (2) a prompt recalled with Up is a surface the `claude-code-2026-10` profile (2.1.317's and 2.1.318's) doesn't record (`─── History 1/1 ───`), refused `Surface`, `cannot_check` until the box is cleared (`inbox/doorbell/010`'s kept-draft history variant flips if a record adds it); (3) U-KB (Q127b): a `keybindings.json` created, changed or removed after the admission's keymap re-read and before Claude Code dispatches the check's byte can let that byte run the newly bound action once; the window is not sub-second (Claude Code picked a changed file up within 2.5 to 3.75 s, sampled) and a stall stretches it; the settlement re-read then holds the rest of the generation (`Hold::Changed`) but cannot undo the action; (4) themes: the recorded palette set (indexed and truecolor dark and light, dark-ansi, light-ansi, dark-daltonized) plus refusal by shape is the boundary, so another theme is refused or unverified; (5) macOS holds a touched box `needs_fetch` until restart (Q134b), and Codex is unarmed until Q35-29. The keys a check holds for Claude's return, and the windows that remain, are the residual below ("Keys a cooked terminal acts on").

**The suspension recovery** (OQ2, `src/composer_probe/recovery.rs`): a suspended doorbell is lifted by the user's own submit or clear, counted over Q35-01's tokenizer (`framing::Token::edit`: CR, `Ctrl+U` or `Ctrl+C` in ground, outside a paste) in the bytes the PTY took, the deck's own bytes fed too so the framing is the agent's. Lifting grants nothing the epoch doesn't: the user's keys moved it, so the next attempt needs a check. The tokenizer reads `ESC P` as a DCS opener, so an Enter after a user's lone `ESC` that the deck's line followed is not counted; that errs toward a longer suspension.

## Accepted residuals

The checks narrow what the deck can type into; they do not close every window. These are accepted, and the user pages promise only what the deck detects:

- **A dialog after the final check.** The pre-Enter recheck reads the frame the evidence state last parsed, then the Enter is queued under the same seam. A dialog the agent opens after that read and before its input loop takes the Enter receives the Enter. Nothing on the deck's side can see that frame in time.
- **A dialog after the second stage's seam.** On a staged line, the rest is queued at a seam that reads the frame again, synchronously, right before the enqueue; that seam is the existing staged-typing check, not something the token-head boundary added. A dialog the agent opens after that read and before it takes the rest receives the rest: the fixed text, whose `n` leaves Codex's folder-trust dialog for another screen and whose letters include keys that move a highlight, and the id's digits, of which `1`, `2` and `3` commit in Codex's update and hooks dialogs. A screen reached that way can receive the remaining bytes before the deck sees it. Compared with a first stage of the token and the fixed text, the token-head boundary makes the first stage eight measured-inert bytes and the second stage longer; it is the same kind of race, and nothing measured shows the overall exposure is smaller. The first ring's settle window, from the release's own slowest measured start-up dialog (6.03 s on Codex 0.164.0, 6.69 s on 0.160.0), keeps rings clear of the start-up dialogs those measurements saw; it does not cover a dialog that opens later. A dialog that opens after the first stage's seam receives only the head, whose bytes are inert in every measured dialog, and the head's missing echo then stops the rest.
- **What the epoch cannot see.** Text the agent puts into its own box (a prompt restored after a blocked submit) moves no epoch, so a box the epoch says is untouched can hold it; the screen check at admission is what stands against it. An exempt report (D3, D3′) moves nothing by design.
- **Between mode samples.** A wrapped pane's input mode is a sample the wrapper took at some moment before the check, refused once it is older than `MODE_MAX_AGE_NS`. A change to the terminal's mode after the newest accepted sample and before the deck's bytes reach the agent is not seen; the transaction stops on a change only once a sample shows it (`ModeChanged`). An unwrapped pane reads the master's termios at each check, so its window is the time between that read and the write.

- **A composer probe's Ctrl+G after its generation retires (A-20d-4, PRD #1666 part 3).** The writer checks a probe's job for retirement once, at its dequeue: its own retirement flag, then the gate's arm section under the gate's lock, then admission (`probe_section` in `src/agent_pty_segments.rs`). A retirement (closing the pane, restarting it, or a spawn over an exited agent) that lands after a successful arm section and before the byte's `write(2)` lets the byte reach that generation's own PTY master. It cannot reach a successor, which has its own master and writer thread, and it releases no held typing, since the retired gate refuses what it held with `exited`. What the retiring agent does with it depends on the editor helper's branch, and the code fixes that branch: retirement drops the generation's records, so nothing publishes a lower count to its arm page, and the page file stays for as long as the daemon runs (a later daemon removes a dead daemon's instance only). A helper the agent runs for the byte therefore reads the arm as present and takes the armed path: it opens no editor, not even a GUI one that would outlive the pane, reports `armed`, and, since a retired generation has no records to consume the invocation, the daemon answers `Uncertain` or the exchange fails; either way the helper prints its one fixed notice into the pane being stopped. The window is between the dequeue check and the write; closing it would mean holding the gate's lock across a PTY write that can block, so no second check was added (Q118b).

- **A person's Ctrl+G whose editor opens its own window (A-20d-12, PRD #1666 part 3).** When a person's Ctrl+G is held behind a check and its helper runs their editor, the segment releases what was typed after the Ctrl+G once the observer sees the editor running (`early_release` in `src/composer_probe/segments.rs`). That release writes to the pane, as typing does when the deck holds nothing: whatever reads the terminal takes it. A terminal editor (vim, nano, `$EDITOR` in the pane) does. An editor that opens its own window (VS Code with `code --wait`, a GUI editor) doesn't read the terminal, so the bytes wait there for the agent's return: Claude Code keeps queued input through its return (P-CRF, which M-PF2 is to confirm) and reads them into its box after the editor's text, and Codex discards input queued while its editor was open as it returns (M31), so they are lost. Both are what each agent does with keys typed into its pane while a GUI editor is open without the deck, so the release keeps that behaviour rather than inventing another: the deck has no firm way to tell a terminal editor from a GUI one (both run as the helper's child with the pane as their terminal, and a process seen blocked reading it is a sample, not a promise), and holding the bytes until the editor ends would keep from a terminal editor the keys typed into it. Nothing is held, so the card shows nothing. What the user page owes (the activation's docs pass): with an editor in its own window, what you type into the agent's pane while it is open reaches Claude Code when you close the editor, and Codex discards it, as without the deck.

- **A person's Ctrl+G whose editor ended before the deck saw it running (A-20d-15, PRD #1666 part 3).** The release above needs the editor seen running. An editor that exits within milliseconds (`code` without `--wait`, an editor script) can end before the observer pins it, or between the pin and the release decision, which re-checks the pin (A-20d-6). Under `Hold` such a segment never completes, so before A-20d-15 everything typed after that Ctrl+G stayed held until the pane's agent restarted. On Claude, the segment now also releases once the editor's end is known by the helper's own account: its report says it reaped the editor and accounted for the tree (`Done` with `Clean` or `Survivors`), and that report was in when the helper's exit drained (`Record::drained_report`; a report delivered only after the drain does not count). That is enough on Claude, since Claude keeps queued input through its return (P-CRF). A drain that gave up uncertain keeps the report it had (`drained_report` is taken at the first drain, however it ended), so a `Done(Clean)` report in by then releases too: the uncertainty bears on the probe's settlement, not on where the bytes go. This release runs on no timer. On Codex it does not apply, and the segment holds until retirement (the revd21 review's U2-1): Codex discards input queued while it returns (M31), so a Codex release would have to wait for Codex's return past the drain frontier (EC's `?2004h`, then `?2026h`), and that frontier is one E-MF's holder scan took, which no review accepts as premise-backed (Q86). A holder of the terminal the scan missed could write those markers while Codex is still away, and the bytes would be lost into its discard with no notice; held, they are refused at retirement and the card shows them meanwhile. Still held until retirement on Claude: a helper that drained with no report, one whose report arrived after the drain, a `Done(Unknown)` tree and a failed start. The release does not complete the segment under `Hold`; what that costs, and what the deck does about it, is the next bullet.

- **A segment that can never complete, and the Ctrl+G behind it (R-HOLD's bounded refusal, PRD #1666 part 3; the user's ruling of 2026-11-03).** Under `Hold`, an `Unarmed` decision opens the writer scope, which only retirement closes, and `Records::complete` refuses every route while the scope is open. So once a person's Ctrl+G during a check is answered by their editor and its segment released as above, that segment stays at the front for the rest of the generation (`Segments::stranded`: released, not cut off, scope open), and nothing behind it can be serialised as a trigger. Before this ruling the cost was: **any** later Ctrl+G in that generation, however long afterwards, was held with everything typed after it until the generation retired (the agent restarting or its pane closing), and the retired gate then refused it all `exited`, so it was discarded, not delivered; and the doorbell's tick counted that front, and the fence it keeps closed, as a check outstanding, so for an `Armable` generation whose box read touched it rang nothing for the rest of the generation. Now the release driver, once its release has gone as far as the next held Ctrl+G behind such a front, refuses that Ctrl+G and every op held after it (`PaneWriter::refuse_stranded`, through `Gate::refuse_held_if` with `GenerationInput::segment_refuse_behind` as its authority under the gate's lock): each op's connection is told `ctrl-g-refused`, which both clients show as `Input not delivered: your Ctrl+G and what you typed after it; the deck passes no more Ctrl+G to this agent until it restarts` (the TUI keeps the pane in its input mode for this reason alone, as the desktop keeps its tile's input for every reason), its ticket settles refused, and no byte of it reaches the PTY. The front stays, and so does its fence, so each later Ctrl+G is cut and refused the same way while that front stays released (an unknown wait's cut-off makes it hold everything instead), and typing without one goes on to the pane as the release lets it. What is refused is what is held at that pass: keys that arrive after it go on. The doorbell's tick no longer counts a stranded front, or its fence, as a check outstanding, and asks no probe for it, since no probe can open a segment behind it (`tick_probe` answers `NotDue`); the ring that follows still types nothing into that box while the fence is closed, since the deck's own line is refused at the writer behind a closed fence (CX-H). Rejected alternatives: refusing at acceptance, so nothing is held at all, which would lose the bytes before the Ctrl+G in the same write, miss a Ctrl+G already held when its front becomes stranded (the decision or drain arriving later), and break the gate's rule that a person's held op is never refused at acceptance; opening the fence behind a stranded front so the ring could type, which breaks the invariant that an open fence means no segment is open (the planner would then pass a Ctrl+G through unnumbered, the alternative the user did not choose) and would type into a box the person touched. Not covered: a front that holds rather than releases (a tool's editor, or a release cut off by an unknown wait) still holds everything until retirement, as before.

- **Keys a cooked terminal acts on, during a Claude check (Q147b, Q151b, Q152b, PRD #1666 part 3).** Claude Code takes its terminal out of raw mode (`ISIG|ICANON|ECHO|IEXTEN`, `ICRNL|IXON`) before it spawns its editor, whatever the helper then decides, and switches back to raw before the write carrying its `?2004h` on return (E1): its editor spawn blocks it, so nothing it writes lands in between (strace of 2.1.317 and 2.1.318, fence28 and fence29). A key the line discipline acts on, written in that window, is not typing: a Ctrl+C is a SIGINT to Claude's process group, which ended the session in act28's lane-2 run. So a Claude segment released on an `Armed` decision (`Segments::front_stop` returns `Stop::Cooked`) releases held input only up to the first `line_discipline_key` (`src/composer_probe/segments.rs`: the `termios(3)` default control characters and Enter under `ICRNL`, read as raw bytes wherever they sit in the input's framing, so an Alt chord or a bracketed paste holding one waits from its last ground point). What it stops at waits, with everything after it, for E1 alone; the helper's exit drain is not waited for, since it says nothing more about Claude's mode and its observer can be starved. Measured on 2.1.317 on a quiet 16-CPU box, 20 trials: decision to E1 2.9 to 7.0 ms (median about 3.6), against about 52 ms while fence28 also waited for the drain. There is no timer: a Claude that never returns holds these keys until retirement, which discards them, and `held_cause` shows "hasn't come back" once the hold is 5 s old. A Claude `Probe` released on `Unarmed` (`Stop::Signal`) runs the person's editor, which they didn't ask for, while Claude is cooked (a GUI editor leaves it cooked for the whole edit), so until that helper's exit drains, the first held op holding a Ctrl+C, Ctrl+\ or Ctrl+Z, unless an op holding a Ctrl+G candidate comes before it, is refused with every op held after it (`refuse_signal`, reason `signal-key-refused`, which keeps the TUI in PaneInput); a terminal editor's own Ctrl+C is refused too, the fail-closed cost. Behind `Cooked`, and behind `Signal` while the pane's termios has `ICANON` (Q160b), the release also stops before the line with no end it has written into the terminal since the front's first release reaches `COOKED_LINE` (4095) bytes, or before a UTF-8 lead byte whose whole character would reach it (`utf8_width`, so the stop never falls between a well-formed character's bytes, revact29 finding 4), counted across release passes on the segment (`Segment::cooked_line`, which `release_held` in `src/agent_pty_segments.rs` reads with its authority and writes back after the release) and ended only by a line feed or, behind `Signal`, Enter's CR (`line_end`; NUL, `VEOL`'s default, is not counted): n_tty's canonical read buffer keeps no more of an unterminated line while nothing reads it (measured on Linux 7.0: 5000 bytes and a line feed written into a cooked pty read back as 4096 bytes). Behind `Signal` each release pass reads the mode under the gate's lock, from the deck's own PTY master (`GenerationDoorbell::canonical`, `PaneWriter::canonical`): a pty has one termios, so the master reads what the slave's last `tcsetattr` set, Claude's cooked mode or the raw mode of a terminal editor it ran (`master_canonical_reads_the_slaves_termios` pins it on a real pty). The check is installed only on panes whose command is `claude` itself (`INSTALL_ROLLOUT`), so that slave is Claude's terminal; a wrapped pane's master, a missing one or a failed read gives `None`. A pass that reads anything but `ICANON` set applies no limit and leaves the count at 0: a raw reader takes what it is given, and n_tty turns what it hasn't read into a line of its own when `ICANON` is set again (measured: 3000 bytes written raw, then `ICANON` set and 3001 more with a line feed, read back as 3000 and 3001). Nothing wakes the driver on a termios change; the next key typed does (`held_accepted`). R-HOLD refuses a Ctrl+G behind a cooked front that can never complete in the same pass (Q123b). Accepted: (a) the window between an `Unarmed` probe's drained exit, or a person's own `HumanG` editor's end (A-20d-15), and Claude's switch back to raw, in which a Ctrl+C can still end the session; E1 can't gate it, since a terminal editor's own `?2004h` would fake one (revfence28 finding 3); and, the count being dropped at the drain (`front_stop` is then `Candidate`), a tail past 4095 bytes held behind an `Unarmed` probe's GUI editor is released in one pass then, and the part of it past 4095 is lost if Claude is still cooked (revact29 finding 3; carrying the count past the drain would need a stop that waits for Claude's return there, which no record gives an `Unarmed` probe); (b) cooked `ECHO` of what the decision releases, a brief flash before Claude redraws; holding every byte while cooked would close it, but `probe_061` requires ordinary typing released at the decision; (c) behind `Unarmed`, the ops held at the decision were typed for Claude's composer but go to the editor the check opened, where vim can read them as commands. Ruled accepted (Q159b, revact29): holding them until the drain (revfence29 finding 2) contradicts `probe_010_a_signal_key_behind_a_checks_unarmed_editor_is_refused`, which requires the letter held at the decision written, and the gate releases in order (`release_until_if` pops from the front), so later ops passing earlier held ones would need a new gate primitive; the user docs say to check the text before leaving the editor; (d) the line limit behind `Unarmed` (Q160b, revact29 finding 1; act29 applied it whatever the mode, so about 4 KB of keys or mouse reports typed into vim without Enter, at 10 to 12 bytes a mouse event under vim's `mouse=a`, held `:wq` and its Enter until retirement): a terminal editor that takes the terminal raw (vim, nano, emacs -nw) gets every key but the refused signal keys, with no line limit, but an editor the check opened that leaves it canonical still has it. A GUI editor's window closes as usual, the held rest going at the drain (with (a)'s tail loss); a line-mode editor in the pane, such as `ed`, given more than about 4 KB without Enter never gets that Enter, so it never exits and the pane is held until it is closed, which `docs/troubleshooting.md` names with its remedy. Also left: a pass that read the terminal raw writes with no limit, so an editor that gives the terminal back to canonical before those bytes land can lose a line's end past 4095, as before act29; a pass that stopped at the limit just before a terminal editor took the terminal raw lets the rest go at the next key typed; a pane whose termios can't be read gets no limit, the GUI-editor tail loss of before act29; (e) refusals take whole ops: R-HOLD refuses what precedes the Ctrl+G in its op (`\x03ab\x07cd` loses `\x03ab`), `refuse_signal` refuses an op such as `ab\x03` accepted between its release and its refusal whole, and a signal key inside a bracketed paste that began in an earlier op refuses that op and leaves the paste's opening op to go on with no end (revfence29 findings 1 and 4); (f) the key set is the kernel's pts defaults, which the deck never changes, so a wrapper that remaps them with `stty` makes it wrong for its pane, and a BSD port would add `VSTATUS` (Ctrl+T) and `VDSUSP` (Ctrl+Y).

- **A probe the writer refuses because the screen moved (A-20d-11, PRD #1666 part 3).** A probe is scheduled from the latest clean frame showing the composer, and the writer checks just before its arm section that no output was taken and no layout change happened since that frame; if either did, it refuses `Admission(Surface)` and writes nothing. That refusal alone counts no failure on the probe schedule (`writer_refusal_counts` in `src/agent_pty_segments.rs`): it says the agent drew in between, not that the box can't be checked, so it neither backs the schedule off nor moves it toward `Broken`, and the next due request schedules from a newer frame. Four in a row (`MOVED_PER_FAILURE`, `Schedule::moved` in `src/composer_probe/probe.rs`) count as one failure, so an agent that keeps redrawing between those two reads is backed off after four asks rather than asked at every doorbell tick for as long as it draws (the revd21 review's U3-1); a counted failure or a read of the box starts the run over. Such a run is measured rare on idle Claude Code 2.1.312 (Q117b). Every other writer refusal still counts, and so does a scheduling-time `Surface` refusal (the frame didn't show the composer). A lapse retry refused at the writer for the screen still counts, since the doorbell tick that asks it counts any refused lapse retry.

## Activation, and what the card and WAKE say

`generation_activation` (`src/doorbell_generation.rs`) decides per generation, from its effective agent type and the release latched from its start-up header:

- no effective agent type, or a type no record measures (OpenCode, Pi, Devin today): `unmeasured_release`, whatever the start-up screen showed;
- then the table in `src/doorbell_activation.rs`, rule by rule: the latch (`unknown_release`, `release_conflict`, `scanner_geometry`, `scanner_budget`), an exact record (complete → `Go`, otherwise its own NoGo, its settle window against `SETTLE_CAP` included), and `unmeasured_release` for every release without a record of its own. There is no admission by version distance (SC-00): a newer release, a patch of a measured one or a pre-release borrows nothing from a nearby record, for Codex as for every other agent, so a release is typed into only once a record measures it.

On the records this branch ships, Claude Code 2.1.310 and 2.1.312 are `Go` and both Codex records stage the line and are `missing_measurement` until their `head_echo` is recorded, so the deck types into those two Claude Code releases and into no other agent. A release with no record of its own is `unmeasured_release`, whatever agent it belongs to (Q15 for Claude Code; SC-00 removed Codex's inheritance by version distance, which had admitted a Codex release up to four minors past its newest complete record). That is the cost the user docs state: after Claude Code updates itself, the doorbell stays off for it until a deck release measures the new version, and `inbox/claude/014` lapses with it (Q17): it reports `SKIP:` naming the installed and the measured releases, which `DOT_AGENT_DECK_REQUIRE_REAL_E2E=1` turns into a failure.

The verdict reaches the card, WAKE and the overdue notice through the re-projection publisher, never from admission directly:

- **One writer.** `reproject` reads the generation's latch cell (`uncertain_input`, `dialog_during_ring`) and its refusal: the activation clause, then the clauses that hold it for the rest of its life (Q18: `input_mode_conflict`, `clock_namespace`, `clock_namespace_unreadable`, `attempt_exhausted`). Transient causes (a stale or cooked mode sample, `input_mode_unverified`, a Dirty frame, the settle window) stay out, so WAKE does not flap while a ring is on its way. Runs are serialised by one mutex, `doorbell_publishing`, shared by the publisher task and a spawn's own run.
- **When it runs.** At spawn, synchronously, before the spawn returns (unlatched, so `unknown_release` until the header is read); then on request: a release latched or changed, the agent type learned from hooks, an `Unresolved` attempt (the transaction waits up to 100 ms for that run), a sticky mode clause first holding the generation, its last attempt number spent, and the cell moving on user or hook evidence. Requests for one generation queued while it waits coalesce; one arriving during its run is queued again behind it. Each run logs `queued_us` and `took_us`, and a run that panics is logged and the publisher goes on.
- **The fence.** The hub write (`InboxHub::set_wake_projection`) happens only while the generation is still the pane's live one, asked under the hub's lock. A generation's exit marks it no longer current before its hub entries go, so a run that read its inputs before an exit and writes after it puts nothing back for the retired generation.
- **What reads it.** `wake_of` picks the route for both the snapshot's WAKE and the overdue notice: an armed waiter → `native`, a turn-end announcement → `turn-end`, a refused typed fallback → `none`, otherwise `doorbell`; the record boundary keeps `none` for a record with no live status. The copy can lag admission by the publisher's latency, in either direction; admission stays the authority, so a stale label never permits or stops typing. A notice already posted keeps the route it was composed with.
- **The log.** When a generation's refusal changes, `doorbell off: the deck does not type into this agent` names the clause and the releases of its agent the deck has measured; the user troubleshooting page points at it.

## Input-mode evidence

An unwrapped pane's agent reads its input from the deck's PTY slave, so the master's termios (a duplicated descriptor held by `GenerationDoorbell`) is its input mode: raw, no echo, or the doorbell types nothing. A wrapped pane's evidence is what the wrapper reports (`src/input_mode.rs`): each sample carries two monotonic stamps, from before and after its `tcgetattr`, refused when they are wider than `MODE_MAX_AGE_NS` apart and aged from the first; a report is accepted only from a process in the session the pane's child leads, bound to the first reporter's `(pid, start time)` (a second one is `input_mode_conflict` for the generation's life), on the daemon's time namespace; the wrapper sends reports only to a daemon identity that answered its capability probe with `input_mode_report`, caching that verdict per identity. An unwrapped pane that a process reports for anyway is read from the store from then on, so a conflict holds it there too.

## The e2e-only seams

- **The stand-in profile.** `STANDIN_PROFILE` (`src/doorbell_screen.rs`) recognises the fixed rows the test stand-ins draw. It is selected by `DOT_AGENT_DECK_TEST_COMPOSER_PROFILE=standin` with `DOT_AGENT_DECK_TEST_COMPOSER_BINARY` naming an absolute path, from the start request's environment or an assignment in its command, the command naming that path; or from the daemon's own environment, for agents the TUI spawns from a configured command a test cannot write. Either way it is typed into only while the pane's process runs that path (`standin_running`, read from `/proc`, so Linux only). None of it is compiled outside the `e2e` feature.
- **The record exclusion.** `DOT_AGENT_DECK_E2E_UNRECORD_CLAUDE_RELEASES` removes Claude Code records by bare release, so `inbox/claude/050` can run a real agent on a release the deck treats as unmeasured. It names Claude Code releases only: since no record feeds another release's verdict (rule 3), removing a Claude record can only turn that release's `Go` into `unmeasured_release`. It stays Claude-only because that is all `inbox/claude/050` needs, and `inbox/doorbell/053` pins that it moves no Codex verdict. A malformed list is refused whole. The production build has a stub that returns an empty list and reads no variable.

## The parsers' work budgets

Two `vt100` parsers run inside the PTY reader's push, under the pane's output lock (`AgentBus::push` in `src/agent_pty.rs`): the release scanner (`src/release_scanner.rs`), which reads a generation's release off its start-up header, and the evidence parser (`src/evidence_state.rs`), which reads the agent's composer. Whatever they cost is time every subscriber of that pane, its resizes and its evidence reads wait for. Most output costs them a few dozen nanoseconds a byte. Some `vt100` operations work over the whole grid, so a pane at 512 × 512 that prints erase-display without pause costs each erase 262 144 cell clears: measured before the budgets, 600 ms of scanner CPU over a 256 KiB window and 19.9 ms of added lock hold per push for the scanner, 18.3 ms for the evidence parser.

### What is counted

Both parsers are fed through `src/vt_work.rs`'s `MeteredParser`, one byte at a time. Before a byte reaches `vt100`, a shadow `vte` tokenizer (the one `vt100` itself runs on, checked by `the_meter_and_vt100_share_one_vte` to be a single version in `Cargo.lock`) prices it in **work units**: 64 for every byte's own parse and check, plus one unit for each cell the operation it completes can write or move. The prices are upper bounds read from `vt100` 0.18.1's `screen.rs`, `grid.rs` and `row.rs`; the module documentation lists them operation by operation. A byte whose price would take the caller past its limit is **not parsed**, and nothing after it is.

The count is of work, not of time, so a loaded machine cannot make a normal launch look expensive, and the bound holds by construction: no push gives a parser more than its limit's worth of units, however the bytes were split, because the check runs before each byte and not after the push.

What a unit costs in time is measured, not derived. `scanner_budget_calibration` in `src/agent_pty_scanner_budget.rs` prices every recorded launch and fifteen pathological traces with no limit and reports their CPU time per unit; `scanner_budget_measurement` replays them through the real push path. On the measuring machine (16 cores, release profile, 2026-10-25) the dearest figures were 0.44 to 0.57 ns per unit for a short recorded launch, varying run to run, 0.43 for the dearest pathological trace priced to its end, and 0.57 for a push that trips a limit through the push path, where a fresh grid's page faults and the push's own work fall inside the same 5 million units. The first pricing runs found three operations priced low, and each was repriced before the limits were set: a full reset (`ESC c`, 2.9 ns per unit, from reallocating every row) and the first switch to the alternate screen (which allocates its grid) now cost `16 × R × C`, and erase-in-line (1.2 ns, erased cell by cell) `4 × C`.

### The limits

| parser | limit | units | at 0.57 ns per unit | the most a recorded launch uses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| release scanner | per push (`SCANNER_WORK_PER_PUSH`) | 5 000 000 | 2.9 ms | 559 680 |
| release scanner | per generation (`SCANNER_WORK_TOTAL`) | 85 000 000 | 48 ms | 12 643 784; a whole 256 KiB window of launches, 19 963 226 (7.8 ms of CPU) |
| evidence parser | per push (`EVIDENCE_WORK_PER_PUSH`) | 5 000 000 | 2.9 ms | none reaches it (`doorbell_044_recorded_launches_stay_within_the_evidence_bound`) |

On the measuring machine the two per-push limits together kept a push's added lock hold under the 10 ms maximum plan §4.6 sets, with room for a machine somewhat slower than that one. The work bound is structural; the time it takes is not (see [What the budgets do not claim](#what-the-budgets-do-not-claim)). The evidence parser has no per-generation limit: it has no byte window and runs for the generation's life, so only its per-push cost is bounded.

A normal launch reaches the per-push limit only in a pane near the cell budget: a first switch to the alternate screen at 512 × 512 costs 4.5 million units on its own, so the read that carries it and a redraw can pass 5 million. That is the case the `scanner_budget` clause tells a user about (make the window smaller and start the agent afresh).

### What a trip does

- **Release scanner.** The scanner is dropped and its window ends (`pane release` says `scanner dropped: work over budget`, and the `release scanner timing` line names the limit, `push` or `total`, beside `work` and `push_work_max`). A generation with no release latched yet reads as `scanner_budget`, which the activation table holds with that clause; a release already latched stays latched.
- **Evidence parser.** The parser is dropped and the state is `WorkOverBudget` (`work_over_budget` in the `pane layout` line), which recognises nothing, so nothing admitted on a Clean frame can be. The next layout edge, a resize or a repaint nudge, starts the evidence over, exactly as after any edge.

Resizes are not charged to either budget. A resize is the deck's own ioctl, not the agent's output, and its cost is recorded separately (`resize_us_max`; up to 6.4 ms measured, to 512 × 512 among other sizes).

### What the budgets do not claim

- The time figures are units times the cost per unit measured on one machine. A machine half as fast spends twice the time on the same units; the work bound is the same everywhere.
- A pane whose every push stays under the evidence parser's per-push limit costs that much on every push for as long as it prints. The bound is per push, as decided, not a rate limit.
- The prices are upper bounds read from `vt100` 0.18.1. A `vt100` update that adds an operation over many cells needs a price for it, and the calibration rerun.
